
[#messages]
== Reading the Messages

The site has three views of the messages: _Details_, _List_ and _Map_ (the map has a chapter of its
own, <<map>>). All three show the same messages, the ones you chose in the filter bar, so you can
move between them without choosing again.

[#choosing-messages]
=== Choosing which messages you see

The filter bar under the menu bar holds the buttons that choose the messages, and the button that
prints them.

.The filter bar
image::PublicSiteFilterBar.png[The filter bar, 365]

* _Navigational Warnings_ and _Notices to Mariners_ switch each kind of message on or off. A button
  that is on is dark. Both are on when you first open the site. One of them is always on: the last
  one that is on cannot be switched off, so to see only notices, switch the warnings off, and the
  other way round. The site remembers your choice in your browser.
* _Show Active Now_ is described in <<active-now>>.
* _Print_ makes a document of the messages you are looking at, ready to print or save (see
  <<printing>>).

[#area-buttons]
Some sites have a second row of buttons in the filter bar, one for each sea area. Click an area to
see only its messages; click it again to see them all. You can choose several areas at once. On a
site that covers more than one region, the row starts with a button for each region, and the site
remembers the region you chose. The Niord base product has no area row of its own: it shows one
only while you browse a publication (see <<browse-publication>>).

[#active-now]
==== Show Active Now, and the dates of a message

_Show Active Now_ keeps only the messages that are in force at this moment: the ones whose dates of
the event include the present moment. A message that gives no such dates is kept, so that no
message is hidden because its dates cannot be judged. Press the button again to see every published
message of the kinds you chose. The site does not remember the button: it is always off when the
site opens. While you browse a publication the button is not there (see <<browse-publication>>),
and _Print_ prints the whole list whether it is pressed or not (see <<printing>>).

The button judges by the dates the authority gave the event, so read the dates in the message
itself to know when it applies:

* The text of the message says when the work, the exercise or the change takes place, for example
  "In the period 1 October - 12 October 2026, dredging will be carried out". Some messages have a
  part called _Time_ for this.
* The line at the end of a message ends with the day the authority published it, for example
  "Published 14 September 2026".

Dates are given in the time zone of the authority that published the message.

[#details-view]
=== The Details view

_Details_ is the view the site opens on. It shows the full text of every message, one under the
other, under the name of the sea area it is in.

.The Details view
image::PublicSiteDetailsPage.png[The Details view, 800]

Each message is a card with all its text (see <<reading-a-message>>). Click its title to open the
message in a window of its own, with its map (see <<message-window>>). The small arrow after the
title shows that it opens.

[#list-view]
=== The List view

_List_ shows one line for each message, under the name of the sea area it is in: the message's
number and its title. It is the quickest way to see what is published for an area.

.The List view
image::PublicSiteListPage.png[The List view, 800]

Click a line to open the message in a window of its own (see <<message-window>>).

[#reading-a-message]
=== Reading a message

A message has these parts, from the top. A part that the message does not have is left out.

.A message in the Details view
image::PublicSiteMessageCard.png[A message in the Details view, 510]

Pictures above the text::
An authority can place a picture or a short video above the text of a message, for example a
section of a chart. It can also place one below the text.

The star (★)::
A star above the number marks a notice based on original information, as printed Notices to
Mariners mark it.

The number::
The message's number, in a grey box, for example _NM-811-26_ or _NW-348-26_. A message without a
number is named by its kind instead, for example _Coastal NW_ or _Temp. NM_. After the number, _(T)_
marks a temporary notice and _(P)_ a preliminary one.
+
A message that the authority has cancelled carries the mark _Cancelled_ beside its number, and one
that has run out carries the mark _Expired_. A permanent notice that has run out has no mark: it
has simply come to the end of its life. The site's own lists hold published messages only, so you
meet cancelled and expired messages in a publication you browse (see <<browse-publication>>), when
you follow a reference from another message, and when you open a link to one message (see
<<message-links>>).

The title::
The title names the country, the sea area, the place and what has happened, for example
_Denmark. Kattegat. Skagen. Light unlit._

References::
Other messages this message refers to, by their number, with what the reference is: _(repetition)_,
_(repetition with new time)_, _(updated repetition)_ or _(cancelled)_. Click a number to open that
message (see <<message-window>>).

Details, Time, Positions, Note, Prohibition, Signals::
The text of the message, in one or more parts. The name on the left says what kind of part it is,
and is written only once for parts of the same kind that follow each other. A part may start with a
short subject in bold.
+
Positions are usually listed in a table, each with a number, for example
`1) 55° 07.432'N - 012° 14.871'E`: degrees and minutes of latitude and longitude. The map in the
message's window marks the same positions with the same numbers. A message may also give a
position on a line of its own, without a number.

Attachments::
Files that belong to the message, such as a chart section or a document. Click _Show attachments_
to list them, and a file's name to open it in a new tab. _Hide attachments_ folds the list away
again.

Charts::
The numbers of the charts the message concerns. An international chart number follows in brackets,
for example _103 (INT 1303)_.

Publication::
The nautical publications the message concerns, such as a list of lights.

The source and the date::
The last line, in brackets, says who reported the matter and when, and the day the authority
published the message, for example
_(Port of Kalundborg 14 August 2026. Published 14 August 2026)_.

[#message-window]
=== The message window

A message opens in a window over the page when you click its title in the _Details_ view, its line
in the _List_ view, or its symbol on the map.

.A message opened from the list
image::PublicSiteMessageDialog.png[A message opened from the list, 800]

The window's title says which kind of message it is, _Navigational Warnings_ or _Notices to
Mariners_. Under it:

* A map of the message's positions, when the message has any (see <<message-map>>). A message with
  a picture placed above its text shows the picture instead.
* The whole text of the message, as described in <<reading-a-message>>.

At the bottom of the window:

* The two arrow buttons step to the previous and the next message of the list you opened the
  window from, so you can read through the list without closing the window. They are not there when
  the message is not in that list, for example when you followed a reference to it.
* _back_ appears when you have followed a reference to another message, and takes you back to the
  message you came from.
* The printer button makes a document of this one message (see <<printing>>).
* _Close_ closes the window. The cross in the top right corner and the kbd:[Esc] key do the same.

[#message-links]
==== Links to one message

Each message has an address of its own, which opens the site with that message's window open, for
example:

----
https://nautiskinformation.brs2.dk/messages/details/message/NM-811-26
----

The last part of the address is the message's number. Send such a link to someone who needs to
read the message, or bookmark it. The link keeps working after the message has been cancelled or
has expired: the window then shows the message with its mark, _Cancelled_ or _Expired_ (see
<<reading-a-message>>), so check the mark before you rely on an old link. When the number is wrong,
or the message has never been published, the window says so: _Message NM-811-26 is not available_.

Closing the window leaves you on the _Details_ view.

[#printing]
=== Printing

The site prints from documents in PDF that the Niord server makes. Each opens in a new tab of your
browser, where you can print it or save it.

* _Print_ in the filter bar makes a document of the list you are looking at: the kinds of message
  you chose, and the areas you chose when the filter bar has area buttons. While you browse a
  publication, it prints that publication's messages. _Show Active Now_ makes no difference to it.
  The button is greyed out while the list is loading, and when the list is empty.
* The printer button at the bottom of a message's window makes a document of that one message.

.The printed list
image::PublicSitePrintPdf.png[The printed list, 498]

The printed list is grouped by sea area, as the _Details_ view is, and says at its top when it was
made.

The server makes only a few documents at a time for the public. When many readers print at the
same moment, the new tab may show an error instead of the document: close it and try again a
little later.

[#when-the-list-does-not-load]
=== When the list does not load

While the site reads the messages, a turning circle shows where they will appear. If the site
cannot read them, for example because the connection was lost, it says so in red: _Failed to load
messages_. That does not mean that there are no messages: reload the page, or try again later.

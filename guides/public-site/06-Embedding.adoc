== Showing the Messages on Your Own Web Site

A harbour, a marina or a sailing club can show the messages on its own web site with the _teaser_:
a small map of the current warnings and notices, with a banner that invites the reader to click it.
A click opens the public site's Map view in a new tab.

.The teaser on another web site
image::PublicSiteTeaser.png[The teaser on another web site, 400]

[#teaser]
=== Adding the teaser to a page

The teaser is the page `/teaser` of the public site, which your page shows in a frame. Add this to
the HTML of your page, where the map should be:

[source,html]
----
<iframe src="https://nautiskinformation.brs2.dk/teaser?lang=en"
        title="Navigational Warnings and Notices to Mariners"
        width="480" height="320" style="border: 0"></iframe>
----

Choose the width and the height to fit your page: the map fills the frame, and places itself so that
the messages are in view.

The address of the teaser takes two settings:

`lang`::
The language of the banner and of the messages, for example `lang=en`, one of the languages the
public site is offered in. Without it, or with a language the site does not offer, the teaser is
normally in the site's own language, so name the language you want.

`area`::
The sea area whose messages the teaser shows, by the area's id in Niord or by its MRN, a name of the
form `urn:mrn:...`. Ask the authority that runs the site which areas it has. Without it, the teaser
shows the messages of the first area the site has, or every message when the site has no areas of
its own.

Settings are joined with `&`, for example `/teaser?lang=en&area=urn:mrn:iho:country:dk`.

The site's operator decides which web sites may show the teaser in a frame. If the frame on your
page stays empty, ask the authority to allow your site.

An address of the teaser written for the earlier public site, such as `/#/teaser?lang=en` or
`/teaser#?lang=en`, still works.


[#introduction]
== Introduction

The public site is where you read the Navigational Warnings and Notices to Mariners that a
maritime authority publishes with Niord. It is written for mariners, harbour offices and anyone
else who needs to know what is going on in the waters the authority covers. You need no account
and nothing to install: the site opens in any up-to-date web browser, on a computer or on a phone,
and you only read on it. Nothing you do on the site changes a message.

The site shows two kinds of message:

* A _Navigational Warning_ (_NW_) tells you about something that matters to navigation now: a light
  that is unlit, a buoy that is missing or out of position, an obstruction, a firing exercise.
* A _Notice to Mariners_ (_NM_) tells you about a change to the charts and nautical publications.
  A notice marked _(T)_ is temporary, for example dredging that goes on for a few weeks; one marked
  _(P)_ is preliminary, a notice in advance of work that has not started yet.

The site lists the messages the authority has published and not yet withdrawn: a message leaves the
site's lists when the authority cancels it or when it expires.

This guide describes the site as the Niord base product makes it. The site of a particular country
may look a little different: its own colours and logo, more languages, and its own links at the
bottom of the page.

[#addresses]
=== Where to find the site

The public site is at https://nautiskinformation.brs2.dk[nautiskinformation.brs2.dk^].

There is also a test site, at
https://nautiskinformation.staging.brs2.dk[nautiskinformation.staging.brs2.dk^], where new versions
of the site are tried out before they are put into use. Its messages are test data: never use them
for navigation. A site that is not set up as a production site says so with a ribbon across its
top left corner, carrying a word such as _TEST_.

Every view of the site has an address of its own, which you can bookmark or send to someone:

[cols="40,60",options="header"]
|===
|Address |What it opens
|`/messages/details` |The full text of every message (the page the site opens on)
|`/messages/table` |The list of messages, one line each
|`/messages/map` |The messages on a map
|`/publications` |The publications you can download
|`/messages/details/message/NM-811-26` |One message, here the notice with the number NM-811-26, in a window of its own (see <<message-links>>)
|===

Addresses written for the earlier public site had a `#` in them, such as `/#/messages/map`. They
still work: the site turns them into the new form when it opens.

[#page-layout]
=== The parts of the page

Every page of the site has the same three parts: the menu bar at the top, the page itself, and a
footer at the bottom.

.The menu bar
image::PublicSiteMenuBar.png[The menu bar, 335]

The _menu bar_ takes you to the four views of the site:

* _Details_ shows the full text of every message, under the name of the sea area it is in.
* _List_ shows one line for each message: its number and its title.
* _Map_ shows the messages on a map.
* _Downloads_ lists the publications you can download, such as the weekly Notices to Mariners.

After _Downloads_ come the language buttons (see <<languages>>). The view you are in is
highlighted.

On the three views of the messages, a second bar under the menu bar, the _filter bar_, chooses
which messages you see and prints them (see <<choosing-messages>>). Both bars stay at the top of the
window while you scroll.

The _footer_ at the bottom of the page carries the site's copyright line. A country's site may add
the authority's name and logo there, and links to its disclaimer, its statement on cookies and its
accessibility statement.

TIP: If you use the keyboard, the first press of kbd:[Tab] on a page shows a link, _Skip to main
content_, which takes you past the menu bar to the page itself. On the views of the messages, the
next press of kbd:[Tab] goes to the first button of the filter bar.

[#languages]
=== Languages

After _Downloads_, the menu bar has one button for each language the site is offered in. A button
shows the language's flag, or the language's name when the site has no flag for it. The flag of the
language you are reading in is brighter than the others. Click a language to read the site in it:
the menu, the buttons and the messages change at once.

The Niord base product is in English only, so its menu bar has one flag. A country's site adds its
own languages.

A message is shown in your language when the authority wrote it in that language. When it did not,
the site shows the message in a language it has, and puts a small flag of that language after the
title, or the language's code in small type, such as _DA_, when the site has no flag for it. So you
can see that the text is not in the language you chose.

[#remembered]
=== What the site remembers

The site remembers some of your choices in your browser, on the device you use, for your next
visit: the language you chose with a language button, and which kinds of message you chose to see
(_Navigational Warnings_, _Notices to Mariners_ or both); on a site that covers more than one
region, also the region you chose. The sea areas you chose are not remembered. These choices stay
in your browser. On another device, or in another browser, the site starts with its own defaults:
both kinds of message, in the site's own language.

A link that names a language, such as one ending in `?lang=en`, opens the site in that language
without changing the language the site remembers for you.

[#position-editor]
== Position Editor

The _position editor_ is where you draw and change the positions of a message part on a map:
single positions (points), lines and areas, with their names. Each message part has its own
positions; see <<positions-editor-field,Positions>> in the Editor chapter.

image::PositionEditor.png[The position editor with an area and three points, 759]

To open it, open a message in the editor, click the *Positions* row of a message part and then
*Edit positions*. The row shows a small map of the part's positions and a numbered list of them,
and the two buttons *Edit positions* and *Edit as text* (<<text-editor>>). The buttons are there
while you can edit the message. The same editor opens from the *Positions* of a template on the
_Execute templates_ page (<<message-template-execution>>). Administrators use it for the extent of
areas and charts too, with the tools that fit there (a *Box* tool among them, and no *Measure*)
and without the names, restrictions and affected areas this chapter describes (see the
link:../admin-manual/manual.html#areas[Areas] and link:../admin-manual/manual.html#charts[Charts]
chapters of the administrator manual).

[[graphical-editor]]The position editor fills almost the whole window:

* The toolbar at the top holds the title, *Positions*, with the help link (the question mark),
  the drawing tools (<<graphical-editor-tools>>), *Undo* and *Redo* (<<position-editor-undo>>),
  the *Actions* menu (the cog, <<action-menu>>), and *Cancel* and *OK*.
* The map shows the positions in magenta, with their names. Drag the map to move it; zoom with
  the mouse wheel or the *+* and *−* buttons. Beside the pointer the map shows the position under
  it, in degrees and minutes.
* The _Positions panel_ floats over the left side of the map: a card for each geometry, with its
  positions (<<features-panel>>). Below it, *Layers* switches the map's layers on and off.

[[action-button-panel]]The editor works on a copy of the part's positions. *OK* puts your changes into the message part
and closes the editor; like every other change in the editor, they are kept only when you save the
message. *Cancel*, or the Escape key, closes the editor and throws your changes away: when you have
changed something, Niord asks first (_Unsaved changes_, "You have unsaved changes. Leave without
saving?"), and *Yes* throws them away. *OK* without a change is the same as *Cancel*. The browser
also warns you when you reload or close the page with changes in the position editor.

[#geojson]
=== Geometries and Their Types

Niord keeps positions in the GeoJSON format, the common format for geographic data on the web;
the link:../model/model.html#niord-geojson-model[message model] describes how Niord uses it. In
the position editor you meet it as the names of the geometry types, which the Positions panel
shows:

[cols="25,75",options="header"]
|===
|Geometry type|What it is

| Point
| A single position: latitude and longitude.

| MultiPoint
| A list of points, kept together as one geometry.

| LineString
| A line: a list of positions joined in order.

| MultiLineString
| A list of lines.

| Polygon
| An area. Its outline is the _Exterior_ ring; an area can also have inner rings, holes in the
  area.

| MultiPolygon
| A list of areas.

| GeometryCollection
| A list of geometries of different types.
|===

Each card of the Positions panel is one _feature_: one geometry together with its name and its
restriction. All the features of a message part make up the part's positions. In practice you work
with points, lines and areas.

[#graphical-editor-tools]
=== Drawing Tools

The tools in the toolbar decide what a click on the map does. Only one tool is chosen at a time;
the editor opens with *Point* chosen, because adding a position is the most common first step.
Whatever the tool, you can drag the map to move it.

[#point-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorPointTool.png[Point, 16] Point

Each click on the map adds a point.

[#path-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorPathTool.png[Line, 16] Line

Click where the line starts and click again for each further position. Double-click to place the
last position and finish the line.

[#polygon-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorPolygonTool.png[Area, 16] Area

Click where the area's outline starts and click again for each corner. Double-click to place the
last corner and finish the area; Niord closes the outline for you.

[#modify-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorModifyTool.png[Select, 16] Select

With *Select* you choose features and change them on the map:

* Click a feature to select it alone. Shift-click adds a feature to the selection or takes it out
  again. Click an empty spot of the map to clear the selection.
* Hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) and drag a box to select every feature the box touches. This works with
  any tool.
* Drag a feature to move all of it.
* On a selected line or area, drag a corner to move it, or drag a point on an edge to add a new
  corner there. Shift-click a corner to delete it.

The selected features are drawn with a heavier outline, and their cards are ticked in the
Positions panel. Selecting decides what the <<action-menu,Actions menu>> offers.

image::PositionGraphicalEditor.png[The selected area with its name and restriction in the Positions panel, 759]

[#delete-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorDeleteTool.png[Delete, 16] Delete

Each click on a feature deletes the feature.

[#measure-tool]
==== image:PositionEditorMeasureTool.png[Measure, 16] Measure

*Measure* draws a measuring line, the same way as *Line*: click for each position, and
double-click to finish. While you draw, a label at the pointer gives the length so far in metres,
kilometres and nautical miles; a finished measurement keeps its label.

image::PositionEditorMeasurement.png[A measuring line with its length, 759]

A measuring line is not a position of the message: it is not saved, and all measuring lines go
away when you choose another tool or close the editor.

[#position-editor-undo]
=== Undo and Redo

image:PositionEditorUndoRedo.png[Undo and Redo, 31] *Undo* takes back your last change in the
position editor, one step at a time, and *Redo* makes it again. The keys do the same: Ctrl+Z to
undo, and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y to redo; on a Mac, Cmd+Z to undo and Cmd+Shift+Z to redo. While
you type in a field, the keys work in the field instead, and while the _Edit as text_ or _Import
geometry_ dialog is open they do nothing in the position editor.

[#action-menu]
=== Actions Menu

image::PositionEditorActionMenu.png[The Actions menu with three points selected, 130]

The *Actions* menu, the cog in the toolbar, holds the actions on all the positions and, below a
line, the actions on the selected features. Only the actions that fit the selection are offered.

[#clear-all-action]
==== Clear all

Deletes every feature. It can be used only when there are positions; *Undo* or *Cancel* brings
them back.

[#zoom-to-extent-action]
==== Zoom to extent

Moves and zooms the map so that it shows all the positions.

[#edit-as-text-action]
==== Edit as text

Opens all the positions as text (<<text-editor>>). Saving the text replaces all the positions in
the editor, as one step that *Undo* takes back.

[#position-editor-import]
==== Import geometry

[[import-action-2]]Adds positions from another source (<<position-editor-import-dialog>>).

[#add-affected-radius-action]
==== Add affected radius

Adds an _affected area_ around the selected features: a circle of the given radius around a point,
a corridor along a line, or a wider area around an area. One or more features of any type can be
selected. See <<affected-areas>>.

image::PositionEditorAffectedRadius.png[An affected radius of 1 nautical mile around a buoy, 388]

[#add-affected-path-action]
==== Add affected path

[[add-affected-radius-action-2]]Adds an affected area along a line through two or more selected
points, in the order of their cards
in the Positions panel. With a radius, the line becomes a corridor of that width on each side.

image::PositionEditorAffectedPath.png[An affected path through three buoys with a radius of half a nautical mile, 388]

[#add-affected-area-action]
==== Add affected area

Adds an affected area with three or more selected points as its corners, in the order of their
cards in the Positions panel. With a radius, the area is widened by that distance.

image::PositionEditorAffectedArea.png[An affected area with three buoys as its corners, 388]

[#merge-geometries-action]
==== Merge geometries

Makes one feature of two or more selected features. Points become a MultiPoint, lines a
MultiLineString and areas a MultiPolygon; a mix of types becomes a GeometryCollection. The names
of the features and of their positions are kept; a restriction is not.

[#split-geometries-action]
==== Split geometries

Makes separate features of each selected feature that is not a single point:

* a line or an area becomes a point for each of its positions;
* a MultiPoint, MultiLineString or MultiPolygon becomes its points, lines or areas;
* a GeometryCollection becomes its geometries.

The names of the positions are kept; the name of the feature that was split is not.

[#convert-action]
==== Convert

With one feature selected, *Convert* changes its type, keeping its positions and names:

* [[convert-to-linestring-action]]*To line* makes a line of a MultiPoint or of an area's outline;
* [[convert-to-polygon-action]]*To area* makes an area of a MultiPoint or a line;
* [[convert-to-multipoint-action]]*To points* makes a MultiPoint of a line or an area.

[[subtract-geometries-action]]*Merge geometries* keeps the areas it joins as they are, side by side
in one feature. Niord has no action that unites areas into one outline, subtracts one area from
another, or gives the part two areas have in common.

[#affected-areas]
=== Affected Areas

An affected area marks the waters a hazard affects: around a wreck, along a cable route, around an
exercise. It is made from the features you selected, its _parent features_, and Niord keeps it in
step with them: when you move or change a parent, the affected area is made again. You cannot move
or reshape an affected area itself, and in the Positions panel its card has a lock and its
positions cannot be changed. When every parent is deleted, the affected area goes too.

Select the affected area's card to set it up:

* *Radius* and the unit beside it (m, km or nm): an affected radius starts at 1 nm. An affected path
  or area starts without a radius; give it one to widen it.
* *Restriction*: what applies in the area. It starts as _Affected_; see <<features-panel>>.
* *Name*: like any feature, an affected area can have a name; until it has one, the panel calls it
  _Affected area_.

[#features-panel]
=== Positions Panel

image::PositionEditorFeaturePanel.png[The Positions panel: the selected area with its restriction and a named position, 200]

The Positions panel lists the features, each on a card. image:PositionEditorCollapse.png[Collapse, 12]
*Collapse* at the top of the panel folds it away, so that you see the whole map; *Expand* opens it
again. A card has, from left to right:

* the handle (*Reorder geometry*): drag a card up or down to change the order of the features.
  The order is the order in which the message numbers its positions;
* the tick box (*Select geometry*), which adds the feature to the selection or takes it out;
* the feature's name, or its geometry type when it has no name: click it to select the feature
  alone;
* *Zoom to geometry*, which moves and zooms the map to the feature;
* *Delete geometry*, which deletes the feature.

When a feature is the only one selected, its card also shows:

* *Name*: the feature's name, one field for each language. A chart system or another client may
  show the name on the map as a label.
* *Restriction*: _None_, _Affected_, _Restricted_, _Speed-restricted_, _Prohibited_ or
  _Stopping-prohibited_. A client may use it to draw the area or to plan a route.
* *Radius*, for an affected area (<<affected-areas>>).

Below that is the feature's geometry as a tree: its type, an area's ring, and its positions in
degrees and minutes. You can change positions here:

* Click a position to change it. Type the new position and press Enter; Escape leaves it as it
  was. A position Niord cannot read is marked red and stays open. You can type
  `55° 10.000'N - 015° 16.000'E`, the same without the symbols (`55 10.000N 015 16.000E`), or
  decimal degrees (`55.1667N 15.2667E`); a comma also works as the decimal sign. Niord does not
  read a position that cannot exist: minutes of 60 or more, more than 90° of latitude or 180° of
  longitude, or a sign before the degrees (the N, S, E or W gives the side). Nor does it read a
  fraction of a degree followed by minutes (`56.5 30N`): write `56.5N` or `56 30N`.
* Point at a position to see its buttons: *+* (_Add a position after this one_), on lines and areas,
  adds a position and opens it for typing; *−* (_Delete this position_) deletes it, as long as
  enough positions are left for the type.
* The arrow to the left of a position (*Edit names*) opens a name for the position, one field for
  each language. Positions of points, lines and an area's outline can have names; a client may show
  them beside the positions.

You can also move through the tree with the keyboard: the up and down arrows go from row to row,
the right and left arrows open and close a row, and F2 or Enter starts typing a position.

[#text-editor]
=== Editing Positions as Text

For positions given as a list, such as positions copied from an e-mail, it is often quicker to
type them than to draw them. *Edit as text* opens the positions as text: from the *Positions* row
of the message part, or from the *Actions* menu in the position editor.

image::PositionTextEditor.png[The Edit as text dialog with an area and three named points, 560]

The text is on the left, and a map on the right shows what Niord reads from it as you type. Below
the text, Niord tells how many features it has read; when it cannot read the text, the text field
gets a red frame. *Show examples* shows two examples of the format. *Save geometry* replaces all
the positions with the ones in the text; it can be used once you have changed the text and Niord
has read it. *Cancel* closes the dialog, asking first when you have changed the text. When you
start from the *Positions* row, saving puts the positions straight into the message part, and you
keep them by saving the message.

The text can show only simple geometries: points, MultiPoints, lines and areas without holes.
When the positions hold anything else, the dialog says "Could not parse the original geometry as
plain text." and the text starts empty. The text carries the positions and their names only:
saving it drops the features' restrictions, and an affected area becomes an ordinary area that no
longer follows its parent features.

[#text-format]
==== The Text Format

The text lists the features one after another, with a blank line between two features:

[source,text]
----
<geometry type>, <feature name>
<position>, <position name>
...
<position>, <position name>

<geometry type>, <feature name>
<position>, <position name>
...
----

* The first line of a feature names its type: `Point`, `MultiPoint`, `LineString` or `Polygon`.
  Without it, a single position is a point and several positions are a MultiPoint.
* Each further line is one position: the latitude, then the longitude, each with its hemisphere
  (N or S, E or W). You can write degrees and minutes (`55° 10.000'N 015° 16.000'E`), degrees,
  minutes and seconds (`55° 10' 30"N`), or decimal degrees, with a point or a comma as the decimal
  sign. A line may start with a number, such as `1)`, and there may be a dash between latitude and
  longitude. Minutes and seconds must be below 60, the latitude at most 90° and the longitude at
  most 180°, and the degrees take no sign: the hemisphere letter gives the side. Degrees followed
  by minutes are whole degrees: Niord does not read `56.5 30N`. Likewise, minutes followed by
  seconds are whole minutes: Niord does not read `56° 30.5' 20"N`.
* For an area, list its corners: Niord closes the outline, so you do not repeat the first corner at
  the end.
* A name follows a comma, after the type (the feature's name) or after a position (the position's
  name). Write a language code and a colon before the name, such as `en:`, to give the name in one
  language, and one such part for each language; a name without a language code is used for every
  language.

[[examples]]A single point:

[source,text]
----
56N 11 30.23E
----

A named area, a named point and a line with named positions:

[source,text]
----
Polygon, en: Exercise area
55° 10.000'N 015° 16.000'E
55° 10.000'N 015° 30.000'E
55° 00.000'N 015° 30.000'E
55° 00.000'N 015° 16.000'E

Point, en: Buoy A
55° 12.500'N 015° 14.000'E

LineString, en: Cable route
1) 55° 05.000'N 015° 09.000'E, en: Landing point
2) 55° 05.000'N 015° 16.000'E
----

[#position-editor-import-dialog]
=== Importing Positions

*Import geometry* in the *Actions* menu adds positions from another source to the ones already in
the editor; it replaces nothing.

image::PositionEditorImportGeometry.png[The Import geometry dialog on the From Area tab, 420]

The dialog has three tabs:

* *From Area*: positions of an area of Niord's base data. Search the areas, or open the tree, and
  click an area that has a geometry; administrators give areas their geometry (see the
  link:../admin-manual/manual.html#areas[Areas] chapter of the administrator manual).
* *From Text*: positions typed in the text format above (<<text-format>>); *Show examples* shows
  two examples.
* *From UTM*: positions in the Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) system, one on each line: the
  zone and its latitude band, then the easting and the northing, such as `32V 624726.15 6207884.60`.
  One position becomes a point and several positions become a MultiPoint. Blank lines are skipped,
  and a comma also works as the decimal sign. When Niord cannot read one of the lines, it reads none
  of them: the text field gets a red frame and nothing can be imported until the line is corrected.
  *Show examples* shows the format and a link to a map of the UTM zones.

Below the tabs, Niord tells how many features it has read. *Import* adds them; it can be used once
there is something to add.

[#drop-files]
==== Dropping a File on the Map

You can also drag a file from your computer onto the map of the position editor: a GeoJSON file, a
KML file (for example from Google Earth) or a file of WKT text. Its features are added to the
positions. A name in the file (a GeoJSON feature's `name` property, a KML placemark's name)
becomes the feature's name in every language.

[#messages]
== Messages

The _Messages_ page lists the NW and NM messages of the current domain that match your filter, in
one of five views. You open it with _Messages_ in the header, which is always there.

image::Messages.png[Messages, 800]

Everyone can open the _Messages_ page, also without logging in. What you see depends on your role:

* Without logging in, or in a domain where you hold no role, you see only the public messages:
  those that are _Published_, _Cancelled_ or _Expired_.
* Logged in with a role in the current domain, you can also find the messages in the other
  statuses, drafts included, with the <<status-filter,Status Filter>>, and you can use the actions
  for your role. A role on the base product's own client, `niord-client-master`, counts in every
  domain.

When you open the page, it lists the published messages of the current domain, sorted the way the
domain is set up to sort them, by area unless a system administrator has chosen otherwise.

Under the header, a bar holds the five views in the middle, _Table_, _Grid_, _Details_, _Map_ and
_Selection_, and at its right the _Filter_ menu and the _Actions_ menu. The panels of the filters
you use appear above the list.

The address of the page in the browser holds your filter. You can bookmark it, or copy it and send
it to someone, and switching between the views keeps the filter.

[#view-modes]
=== View Modes

The tabs in the middle of the bar choose how the messages are shown:

image::ViewModes.png[View Modes, 324]

[#table-view-mode]
==== Table View Mode

_Table_ is the view the page opens in. It lists the messages in a compact table (see the example at
the start of this chapter), with these columns:

* _ID_: the message's ID, coloured by its status. A message that has no ID yet, such as a draft,
  shows its type instead, for example _Coastal NW_.
* The dates: the event dates of the messages. Click the arrow next to the column's heading, _Select
  date column_, to show the _Publish date_ or the _Follow-up date_ instead. The browser remembers
  your choice.
+
image::DatesColumnMenu.png[Select Date Column, 191]
* _Title_ and _Areas_.
* The actions, under a cog: each message's cog is the menu of actions for that one message (see
  <<message-details>>).
* The selection, under a star: each message's star selects it (see <<message-selection>>). The star
  in the heading selects every message the list has loaded, or clears the selection when some are
  selected.

Click the heading _ID_, the date heading or _Areas_ to sort the list by that column. Click the same
heading again to switch between ascending and descending order; an arrow shows the order. The
_Title_ column cannot be sorted.

While the list is sorted by area, a heading row names each area, and an editor finds a _sort…_
link next to it (see <<sort-area-action>>).

A red bar along the left edge of a row means that the message needs attention: its follow-up date
has passed. Point at the row to read _Requires attention (e.g. expired/overdue warning)_. The bar
marks the rows in the _Details_ view too; a selected row shows its selection colour instead.

image::FollowUpOverdue.png[Rows Whose Follow-up Date Has Passed, 784]

[#grid-view-mode]
==== Grid View Mode

In the _Grid_ view, each message is a card with a thumbnail map of the message, its ID, its title
and its text. The view helps you recognise a message by its place on the map. Point at a card to
see its selection star in the middle, and use the cog in its corner for its actions.

image::GridViewMode.png[Grid View Mode, 800]

[#details-view-mode]
==== Details View Mode

In the _Details_ view, each message is shown in full, the way it is shown in the
<<message-details,Message Details>> panel, with a thumbnail map at its left and its cog at its
right. Point at the thumbnail map to see the message's selection star. A message marked as original
information shows a star above its text.

image::DetailsViewMode.png[Details View Mode, 800]

[#map-view-mode]
==== Map View Mode

In the _Map_ view, the messages are drawn on a map that fills the window. The map is a filter of
its own: the list holds only the messages within the part of the map you see, and moving or
zooming the map searches again. The _Map area_ panel at the top left shows the corners of that part
of the map.

Below it, the _Search Result_ panel lists the messages in the map. It is collapsed when you open
the view; click the arrow in its heading to expand it. Its heading reads _Partial Search Result_
when the map holds more messages than were loaded. Point at a message in the list to highlight it
on the map, and click the magnifying glass next to it to zoom in on it. Messages without a position
are listed too, without a magnifying glass.

image::MapViewMode.png[Map View Mode, 800]

Point at a message on the map to see its title, and click it, on the map or in the list, to open
its details. In the _Map_ view a click never selects a message. The _Layers_ panel at the bottom
left switches the map's layers on and off; when you filter on charts or areas, their outlines are
layers of their own.

[#selection-view-mode]
==== Selection View Mode

The _Selection_ view lists the messages you have selected, in the same way as the _Details_ view.
See <<message-selection>>.

[#load-more]
==== Long Lists

A long list is loaded a part at a time: 200 messages at a time, and up to 1000 in the _Map_ view.
When more messages match than are shown, a button at the bottom of the list says which ones it will
load, for example _Load 200 - 400 of 813 messages_:

image::LoadMore.png[Load More Messages, 774]

If loading fails, click _Retry_ to try the same part again without losing what is already shown.

[#opening-a-message]
==== Opening a Message

Click a message's title in the _Table_ view, or the message itself in the other views, to open it
in the <<message-details,Message Details>> panel. The messages are links: a click with the middle
mouse button, or with kbd:[Ctrl] held down, opens the message in a new browser tab instead.

[#hold-e-to-edit]
If you hold down the kbd:[E] key while you click a message in the _Table_, _Grid_, _Details_ or
_Selection_ view, the message opens straight in the editor. This works for every message you may
open in the editor; any other message opens in the details panel as usual. On some laptop touchpads
a tap is ignored while a key is held down; a mouse click always works.

[#message-selection]
=== Message Selection

In every view but the _Map_ view, each message has a star,
image:SelectionButton.png[Selection, 16]. Click it to select the message, and click it again to
deselect it. In the _Table_ view the star is in the last column; in the _Grid_, _Details_ and
_Selection_ views it shows in the middle of the message's thumbnail map when you point at it, and
stays there while the message is selected. The star in the message details panel does the same.

The selected messages are listed in the _Selection_ view. Its tab shows how many messages are
selected, and the cross next to the number clears the selection:

image::MessageSelection.png[Message Selection, 800]

The selection is kept while you filter, search and switch between the views, so you can build it
up from several searches. It is emptied when you reload the page, choose another domain, or log in
or out. Print, mail or tag the selection before you do any of these.

The selection lets you act on just the messages you choose rather than on everything the filter
finds: you can print them, mail them, add them to a tag, compare two of them, export them and
change their status. These actions are described in <<message-actions>>.

[#message-filtering]
=== Message Filtering

The _Filter_ menu lists the filters. Click a filter to switch it on: its panel appears above the
list, where you set its value. The menu stays open, so you can switch on several filters in turn,
and a dot marks each filter that is on. The number on the _Filter_ button counts them.

image::MessageFiltering.png[Message Filtering, 800]

Your saved filters head the menu, such as _Guide example: Baltic drafts_ in the picture
(see <<save-filter>>).

The list follows every change at once, and the address of the page in the browser changes with it,
so you can bookmark a filter.

To switch a filter off, click the cross at the right of its panel, or click the filter in the
menu again. Either way its value is cleared.

The filters are described one by one below.

[#domain-filter]
==== Domain Filter

image::MessageFilterDomain.png[Domain Filter, 138]

The _Domain_ filter searches the messages of another domain without changing your current domain.
This is useful when, for example, you want to base a new Notice to Mariners on a copy of an
existing Navigational Warning.

When you search another domain, only its public messages are found: those that are _Published_,
_Cancelled_ or _Expired_.

Like every filter, the _Domain_ filter is kept in the address of the page, as `searchDomain=`
followed by the id of the domain, so a bookmark or a link with the filter set searches that domain
and leaves your current domain as it is.

[#message-series-filter]
==== Message Series Filter

image::MessageFilterSeries.png[Message Series Filter, 313]

The messages of a domain always belong to one of the domain's _message series_, which decide among
other things how the messages are numbered. A domain for navigational warnings may, for example,
have one series for local warnings and another for coastal warnings.

The _Message series_ filter finds the messages of the series you pick. With the _Domain_ filter on,
you pick from the series of that domain.

[#text-filter]
==== Text Filter

image::MessageFilterText.png[Text Filter, 188]

The _Text_ filter, whose panel is labelled _Search_, finds the messages that contain the words you
type in _Search terms_. It searches all the texts of a message: its ID, title, details and
subjects, areas, categories, charts, references, publication and source.

When you are logged in, you can use this syntax:

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
|Example|Finds

|`light buoy`|Messages with at least one of the words.

|`"light buoy"`|Messages with the words next to each other, in this order.

|`+light -buoy`|Messages that contain _light_ and do not contain _buoy_.

|`harb*`|Messages with a word that starts with _harb_, such as _harbour_.
|===

Without logging in, the text is searched as plain words.

A system administrator can switch the text search to the database's own search (the setting
_disableLucene_). The text filter then finds the messages that contain all the words you type, also
in other forms of the words (_harbour_ finds _harbours_), and a `*` is not understood.

For some minutes after the server has been restarted, the text filter searches an index that is
still being built, and messages may be missing from the result. The list then shows a warning above
the result, until the index is complete. A print of the list made in those minutes says so in a line
at the top of the PDF, and so does a report run with _Report..._ (<<report-action>>); the scheduled
mails of a mailing list that searches by text wait for the index instead, for up to half an hour
after the restart. The mail dialog shows the same warning above its fields, and an export first
shows it and asks whether to export all the same. This does not happen with the database's own
search (_disableLucene_), whose index is kept when the server restarts.

[#type-filter]
==== Type Filter

image::MessageFilterType.png[Type Filter, 186]

The _Type_ filter finds messages of the types you pick. Click a type to pick it, and click it again
to leave it out; _All_ picks every type and _Reset_ none.

For Navigational Warnings the types are _Local_, _Coastal_, _Sub-area_ and _Navarea_; for Notices
to Mariners _Preliminary_, _Temporary_, _Permanent_ and _Misc_. The filter offers only the types the
domain's message series use. In a domain with both Navigational Warnings and Notices to Mariners, it
also has the choices _All_, _NW_ and _NM_.

Beside the types of Navigational Warnings, _Cancellation warnings_ lists the cancellation warnings
Niord published when warnings were cancelled (<<cancellation-warning>>) instead of the warnings.
Click it again to list the warnings.

[#status-filter]
==== Status Filter

image::MessageFilterStatus.png[Status Filter, 368]

When no status is picked, the page shows the published messages. The _Status_ filter lets you pick
any combination of statuses: _Published_, _Draft_, _Verified_, _Cancelled_, _Expired_ and
_Deleted_. _All_ picks every status and _Reset_ none.

When you are logged in with a role in the current domain, the _Tag_ and _Publication_ filters are
an exception: with either of them on and no status picked, the list holds the messages of the tag
or the publication in every status, drafts and deleted messages included.

Every status is offered when you are logged in with a role in the current domain. Without logging
in, in a domain where you hold no role, or with the _Domain_ filter set to another domain, only the
public statuses are offered: _Published_, _Cancelled_ and _Expired_.

[#tag-filter]
==== Tag Filter

image::MessageFilterTag.png[Tag Filter, 309]

_Message tags_ are named groups of messages; see <<message-tags>>. The _Tag_ filter finds the
messages of the tags you pick. Type part of a tag's name in _Search tags_ and pick the tag, or
click image:TagButton.png[Browse all tags, 16], _Browse all tags_, to choose from a list of the tags
(see <<tag-picker>>).

[#publication-filter]
==== Publication Filter

image::MessageFilterPublication.png[Publication Filter, 321]

Administrators publish _publications_ made from messages, such as the weekly Notices to Mariners.
The _Publication_ filter finds the messages a publication holds. Type part of its title in _Search
publications_ and pick it from the list.

If a publication cannot be used as a filter, the panel says so under the field, starting with _The
publication filter could not be applied_.

[#promulgation-filter]
==== Promulgation Filter

image::MessageFilterPromulgation.png[Promulgation Filter, 307]

System administrators set up _promulgation types_, such as NAVTEX, which are the channels a message
is sent through; see <<promulgation-editor-field,Promulgation Editor Field>>. The _Promulgation_
filter finds the messages that use the promulgation types you pick.

[#user-filter]
==== User Filter

image::MessageFilterUser.png[User Filter, 509]

The _User_ filter finds the messages a user has worked on. Pick the user in _Search users_, or click
_Me_ for yourself, and choose how: _Created by_ (the user created the message), _Updated by_ (the
user has changed it at some point) or _Last updated by_ (the user made the latest change).

The _User_ filter is offered only when you are logged in.

[#comments-filter]
==== Comments Filter

image::MessageFilterComments.png[Comments Filter, 419]

Writing a message often involves several people who write it and read it through. To help them,
users can add _comments_ to a message and acknowledge the comments of others; see the editor's
<<comments-editor-page,Comments>> page.

The _Comments_ filter finds messages by their comments: _Any_ (messages with comments), _Own_
(messages you have commented on), _Any unacknowledged_ and _Own unacknowledged_ (messages with
comments nobody has acknowledged yet). _Ignore comments_ leaves comments out of the search.

[#reference-filter]
==== Reference Filter

image::MessageFilterReference.png[Reference Filter, 223]

A message may refer to other messages. Each reference has a type that says, for example, that the
message cancels the referenced message or repeats it.

The _Reference_ filter finds messages by these references. Start typing in _Message ID_ (at least
three characters) and pick the message: the list of suggestions says whether your text matched the
message's database ID, its short ID or its text. That message is the starting point, and the number
field next to it says how many levels of references to follow, from 0 to 5. It starts at 1:

* With 0, only the message itself is found.
* With 1, the messages that refer to it, or that it refers to, are found as well.
* With 2, also the messages that refer to those, or that those refer to, and so on.

[#chart-filter]
==== Chart Filter

image::MessageFilterChart.png[Chart Filter, 279]

The _Chart_ filter finds the messages that concern any of the sea charts you pick in _Search
charts_. In the _Map_ view, the charts' outlines are shown on the map.

[#area-filter]
==== Area Filter

image::MessageFilterArea.png[Area Filter, 289]

Administrators keep the areas in a tree, from countries down to waters and places. The _Area_ filter
finds the messages of the areas you pick, including those of their sub-areas. Type part of an
area's name in _Search areas_, or click the magnifying glass, _Browse area tree_, to pick areas in
the tree. In the _Map_ view, the areas' outlines are shown on the map.

[#category-filter]
==== Category Filter

image::MessageFilterCategory.png[Category Filter, 314]

Categories, such as _Light_ or _Light buoy_, are also kept in a tree by administrators. The
_Category_ filter finds the messages of the categories you pick, including those of their
sub-categories. Type in _Search categories_, or click _Browse category tree_.

[#date-filter]
==== Date Filter

image::MessageFilterDate.png[Date Filter, 346]

The _Date_ filter finds messages by a date. Choose which date in the list at the left, and set
_From_, _To_ or both:

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
|Date|Finds the messages

|Publish date|whose publishing period overlaps the dates.

|Publish from date|that were published, or are to be published, between the dates.

|Event dates|whose event dates overlap the dates.

|Created date|that were created between the dates.

|Updated date|that were last changed between the dates.
|===

[#clear-filter]
==== Clear Filters

_Clear Filters_ at the bottom of the _Filter_ menu switches every filter off.

[#save-filter]
==== Save Filter

_Save Filter..._ at the bottom of the _Filter_ menu saves the filters you have set, so you can use
them again. Give the filter a name in the _Save filter_ dialog and click _Save_.
Saving under the name of one of your saved filters, whatever its capital letters, replaces that filter.

image::SaveFilterDialog.png[Save Filter Dialog, 240]

Your saved filters are listed at the top of the _Filter_ menu. Click one to set its filters again,
or click the bin next to it to delete it. Saved filters are yours: nobody else sees them.

Without logging in, _Clear Filters_ and _Save Filter..._ are greyed out and cannot be used.

[#message-actions]
=== Message Actions

The _Actions_ menu at the right of the bar lists what you can do with the messages the filter has
found, or with the messages you have selected.

image::MessageActions.png[Message Actions, 167]

Which actions the menu holds depends on your role and on whether you have selected messages:

* Everyone can print the list, select all messages, compare two selected messages and sort the
  list.
* When you are logged in, you can also export the messages the filter has found.
* When you are logged in with a role in the current domain, you can also print and export the
  selection, run a report, tag messages and manage tags.
* Editors can also mail the selection, change the status of the selected messages and sort the
  messages of an area.
* Administrators can also import messages.

The actions are described one by one below.

[#print-messages]
==== Print Action

image::PrintDialog.png[Print Dialog, 360]

_Print..._ prints the messages the filter has found, as a PDF. The print dialog, _Print 2
messages_ for two messages, has these settings:

* _Output language_: the language the messages are printed in.
* _Report_: the layout of the PDF. The reports are set up per domain by a system administrator.
  Usually there is a _Standard_ report, which prints the details of the messages, and a _Draft_
  report, which prints every language of the messages with more of their fields, useful for reading
  messages through. A domain for Notices to Mariners often has more reports, for example for the
  weekly Notices to Mariners.
* _Layout_ (_portrait_ or _landscape_) and _Paper size_.
* _include thumbnails_, a thumbnail map beside each message. It is offered only when the report
  leaves the choice to you.
* Some reports have settings of their own, listed below the others by name.

Click _Print_ to make the PDF; the browser opens it. The browser remembers the layout, the paper
size and the thumbnails for the next time; the report and its settings start afresh each time.

[#print-selection-action]
==== Print Selection Action

_Print selection..._ works like the <<print-messages,Print Action>>, but prints only the messages
you have selected.

[#report-action]
==== Report Action

image::MailingListReportDialog.png[Mailing List Report Dialog, 520]

Niord can send reports to the recipients of a mailing list on a schedule; see the
link:../admin-manual/manual.html#mailing-lists[Mailing Lists] chapter. Sometimes you need such a
report outside its schedule.

_Report..._ opens the dialog _Execute Mailing List Report_, which lists the reports that may be run
on demand, with their schedules. Click _Execute_ next to a report to see its result below the list.
To print the result, or keep it open, click _Open in new window_.

[#e-mail-selection-action]
==== E-mail Selection Action

image::MailDialog.png[E-mail Dialog, 360]

_E-mail selection..._, for editors, mails the messages you have selected. The dialog _Mail 2
selected messages_ asks for:

* _Output language_: the language of the messages in the mail.
* _To_: the recipients. Type in _Select e-mail addresses_ and pick from the list: part of a name
  finds Niord's users with that name, and a whole e-mail address is offered as it is, so you can
  also mail someone who is not a user of Niord.
* _Subject_, which is required, and an optional _Message_ to the recipients.

Click _Send Mail_ to mail the details of the selected messages. This can be useful when, for
example, you follow up on the work a Notice to Mariners describes. Niord queues the mail and sends
it shortly after, as the message on the screen says. _My mails..._ in the same menu shows whether it
was sent (see <<my-mails-action>>), and Niord warns you when a mail of yours could not be sent after
its retries.

[#my-mails-action]
==== My Mails Action

_My mails..._ lists the mails you have sent in the last 7 days, with _E-mail selection..._ or with a
comment (see <<comments-editor-page>>), newest first: when each was created, its subject, its
recipients and its status:

* _Sent_: the mail has been handed to the mail server.
* _Waiting_: the mail is queued and will be sent within a minute or two.
* _Retrying_: an attempt failed, and Niord tries again, at longer and longer intervals, for about
  an hour and twenty minutes. The last error is shown below the status.
* _Held, not delivered_: the mail filter of this deployment kept the mail from its recipients, so
  it did not reach them, or not all of them. A system administrator can send it again.
* _Not sent_: the last attempt failed too, and Niord gives up. The last error is shown below the
  status; reach the recipients another way.

While you work in Messages or in the editor, Niord looks at your mails every five minutes and shows a
warning, which stays until you close it, when one of them could not be sent. It does not look while
the browser tab is hidden or while you are elsewhere in Niord, and looking does not keep you signed
in.

[#add-selection-to-tag-action]
==== Add Selection to Tag Action

_Add selection to tag…_ opens the tag picker (see <<tag-picker>>), where you tick the tags the
selected messages should be added to.

[#remove-selection-from-tag-action]
==== Remove Selection from Tag Action

_Remove selection from tag…_ opens the tag picker with only the tags the selected messages are in.
Untick a tag to take the messages out of it.

[#manage-tags-action]
==== Manage Tags Action

_Manage tags…_ opens the tag manager, where you create, change and delete tags. See
<<tag-manager>>.

[#select-all-action]
==== Select All Action

_Select all_ selects every message the list has loaded. In a long list, that may be fewer than the
filter has found (see <<load-more>>). See <<message-selection>>.

[#clear-selection-action]
==== Clear Selection Action

_Clear selection_ empties the selection. See <<message-selection>>.

[#compare-messages]
==== Compare Action

image::ComparisonDialog.png[Message Comparison Dialog, 560]

_Compare two messages_ is offered when you have selected exactly two messages. It opens the dialog
_Compare messages_ on its _Side-by-side_ tab, which shows the two messages next to each other, each
the way the details panel shows it.

_Message 1_ and _Message 2_ name the two messages. When the dialog opens from your selection, they
show the messages' database IDs, the long codes of letters and digits that Niord gives every
message. To compare another message, clear a field with the cross on its chip, type at least three
characters of the message's short ID, such as _NW-348_, and pick the message from the list. Deleted
messages can be picked too. _Swap messages_ changes their places, _Language_ chooses which language
to compare, and _Show XL_ makes the dialog larger.

The _Changes_ tab shows the text of the two messages as one, with the words of message 2 that
message 1 does not have marked as _Additions_ and the words of message 1 that message 2 does not
have as _Deletions_:

image::ComparisonDialogChanges.png[Message Comparison Changes, 560]

The _Data_ tab compares the data of the two messages, field by field, and marks what was changed,
added and removed. Click the name of a group of fields to fold or unfold it; in the picture,
_areas_ and _categories_ are folded. The tab is meant for advanced users:

image::ComparisonDialogData.png[Message Comparison Data, 560]

[#export-action]
==== Export Action

_Export..._ downloads the messages the filter has found as a zip archive. The archive holds the
messages, a page that shows them, and the files attached to them, such as the pictures of a Notice
to Mariners. It can be read back into Niord with the <<import-action,Import Action>>. An archive holds at
most 1,000 messages: when the filter has found more, Niord says so as the download starts, and the archive
holds the first 1,000 in the order of the list.

[#export-selection-action]
==== Export Selection Action

_Export selection..._ works like the <<export-action,Export Action>>, but exports only the messages
you have selected.

[#import-action]
==== Import Action

image::ImportDialog.png[Message Import Dialog, 440]

_Import..._ is offered to administrators only, because it can change many messages at once. It
reads a zip archive made by the <<export-action,Export Action>>. Click _Choose file_, or drop the
archive on the dialog, and choose how to import it:

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
|Setting|Choices

|Message UIDs|_Ignore messages with existing UIDs_ leaves out each message whose unique id is
already in Niord; _Assign new UIDs to all messages_ imports every message as a new one.

|Message status|_Assign DRAFT status to all messages_, or _Preserve status for messages_.

|Areas, categories and charts|When a message names an area, a category or a chart that Niord does
not have: _Ignore non-existing areas, categories and charts_, or _Create non-existing areas,
categories and charts_.

|Message series|_If valid, preserve associated message series_ keeps a message's series when it
belongs to the current domain; _Always assign the default series below_ puts every message in the
_Default message series_.

|Tag|When you pick a tag, every imported message is added to it.
|===

Click _Import_ to start. The import runs as a batch job in the background: the dialog shows the
answer under _Result_, with a _View batch job_ link to follow the job. The link opens the batch job
page of _Sysadmin_, so it works for system administrators only; anyone else is told _Access denied._
and taken to the _Home_ page. The list of messages is not updated by itself; search again when the
job has finished.

[#update-selection-status-action]
==== Update Selection Status Action

image::StatusUpdateDialog.png[Status Update Dialog, 520]

_Update selection status…_ is offered to editors when messages are selected. It changes the status
of many messages at once, which you otherwise do one message at a time on the editor's
<<status-editor-page,Status>> page.

The dialog _Update Statuses_ lists the selected messages whose status can change, with their
_Current status_. For each, pick the _New status_ among the changes its status allows:

* a _Draft_ can become _Verified_ or _Deleted_;
* a _Verified_ message can become _Published_, _Draft_ or _Deleted_;
* a _Published_ message can become _Cancelled_.

_Cancelled_, _Expired_ and _Deleted_ messages cannot change status and are left out. To make the
same change for every message it fits, pick it in _Change all statuses_, for example _Draft →
Verified_. Click _Update_ and confirm to change the statuses.

The statuses change together or not at all. If one of the changes is refused, for example because
another editor changed that message after you opened the dialog, no status is changed, a message
names the message and the reason, and the dialog stays open.

[#sort-area-action]
==== Sort Area Action

image::SortMessagesDialog.png[Sort Messages by Area Dialog, 480]

_Sort area…_, offered to editors, sets the order of the messages within an area, which is the order
they are listed and printed in when sorted by area.

In the dialog _Sort Messages_, pick the _Area_ and the status of the messages to sort, _Published_,
_Verified_ or _Draft_. The messages are listed in their current order. Drag a message by its handle,
image:DragHandle.png[Drag Handle, 16], to its place. Each move is saved at once. Click _Close_ when
you are done; the list of messages is then updated.

You can also click _sort…_ next to an area's heading in the list, which is shown while the list is
sorted by area:

image::SortAreaLink.png[Sort Messages by Area Link, 153]

The dialog then lists the messages under that heading, whatever their status.

By default, the messages of an area follow the order of the area tree, which administrators keep:

image::AreaTree.png[Sample Area Tree, 251]

An administrator can also give an area a _Message sorting_, so that its messages, and those of its

The _Sort area…_ action then lets you adjust that order by hand.

[#message-list-sort-selection]
==== Sorting the List

The last items of the _Actions_ menu sort the list: _Areas_, _ID_, _Event dates_, _Publish date_ and
_Follow-up date_. Choosing the field the list is already sorted by switches between ascending and
descending order. An arrow next to the field shows the current order.

[#message-details]
=== Message Details

image::MessageDetailsDialog.png[Message Details, 800]

The _Message_ panel opens on the right of the page when you open a message from a list (see
<<opening-a-message>>). The address of the page then names the message, so you can copy it and send
it to someone.

At the top, a map shows the message's positions and areas, with the _Layers_ panel to switch its
labels on and off. The map is left out when the message has no position, or when a picture attached
to the message is shown above it. Below the map, the message is shown in full, with its promulgation
when it has any.

At the bottom of the panel:

* The arrows show the previous and the next message of the list. When you click a referenced message
  in the text, the panel shows that message instead, and a _Back_ arrow takes you back.
* The star selects or deselects the message.
* The cog, _Message actions_, is the menu for this one message:
** _Edit_ opens the message in the editor. It is offered when you are logged in and the message
   belongs to the current domain; a user who is not an editor can read the message there and comment
   on it.
** _Copy_, for editors, starts a new message in the editor as a copy of this one, once you confirm
   with _Copy Message_ in the dialog _Copy Message?_. For a Notice to Mariners, the dialog also
   offers _Include reference to copied message_.
** _Print_ prints this message, with the same dialog as the <<print-messages,Print Action>>. It is
   offered to everyone.
** _Add to tag…_ opens the tag picker for this message. _Add to_ followed by a tag's name adds the
   message to the tag you used last, in one click. _Remove from…_ lists the tags the message is in,
   to take it out of one. These items are offered when you are logged in with a role in the current
   domain.
* The question mark opens this section of the manual, and _Close_ closes the panel.

[#message-tags]
=== Message Tags

_Message tags_ are named groups of messages, for example the messages a colleague should read
through, or the messages of a project. They are used in the <<tag-filter,Tag Filter>>, in the
<<import-action,Import Action>>, and by the actions that add messages to tags and remove them.

A tag is one of three kinds:

* A _private_ tag is yours: only you see it and use it.
* A _domain_ tag belongs to a domain and is shared by everyone who works in that domain.
* A _public_ tag is shared by every domain.

A tag can have an expiry date; within an hour after it, Niord deletes the tag, but not its
messages. A tag can also be _locked_: no messages can be added to a locked tag or removed from it,
and only an administrator can unlock it.

[#tag-picker]
==== The Tag Picker

The tag picker opens where you click: from _Add selection to tag…_ and _Remove selection from
tag…_ in the _Actions_ menu, from _Add to tag…_ in a message's cog, and from _Browse all tags_ in
the _Tag_ filter, where its title is _Filter by tag_.

image::TagPicker.png[The Tag Picker as the Tag filter opens it, 270]

The chips at the top, _private_, _domain_ and _public_, show only the tags of those kinds; with no
chip chosen, every kind is shown. In the picture, _private_ and _domain_ are chosen. _Search tags_ finds a tag by its name. Each tag shows how many
messages it holds.

When you add messages to tags, a tick shows that all the messages are in the tag, and a dash that
some of them are. Ticking or unticking a tag adds the messages to it, or takes them out, at once.
Locked tags are not listed then, since they cannot change. In the _Tag_ filter, ticking a tag
filters the list by it instead, and locked tags, marked with a lock, are listed too. _Manage tags…_
at the bottom opens the tag manager, and _Done_ closes the picker.

[#tag-manager]
==== The Tag Manager

_Manage tags…_ in the _Actions_ menu, or in the tag picker, opens the _Message Tags_ panel, where
you create, change and delete tags.

image::MessageTagsDialog.png[Message Tags, 800]

The panel lists the tags you can see, with their _type_, the date they were _created_, their
_expiry date_ and the number of messages they hold. Click a column heading to sort by it. _Name_
and the chips _private_, _domain_ and _public_ narrow the list.

* _New Tag_ adds a row at the top of the list. Type the _Tag Name_, choose who shares it (private,
  domain or public), set an _Expiry Date_ if the tag should be deleted by itself, tick _Locked_ if
  its messages should not change, and click the tick to save it.
* _Edit tag_ (the pencil) changes the tag's sharing, expiry date and lock. A tag's name cannot be
  changed once it is created.
* _Copy tag_ adds a new row filled in from the tag, without its lock, for you to name and save.
* _Delete tag_ (the bin) deletes the tag after you confirm. Its messages are not deleted.
* _clear_, next to the number of messages, empties the tag without deleting it.

A locked tag shows a lock instead of these actions. An administrator can click the lock, _Unlock
tag_, to unlock it.

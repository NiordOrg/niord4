[#firing-exercise-schedule]
== Firing Area Schedule

Firing areas are the sea areas where the armed forces hold firing exercises. Mariners are warned
of each exercise: where it takes place and when. In Niord, editors keep a _schedule_ of the times
each firing area is in use, the _firing periods_, and Niord turns the schedule into the warnings by
itself: the _firing exercise_ messages.

[#firing-schedule-setup]
=== How It Fits Together

A system administrator and an administrator set the parts up once; the system administrator's
side is described in the link:../sysadmin-manual/manual.html#firing-schedules[Firing Schedules]
chapter of the system administrator manual.

* The firing areas are areas of Niord's base data of the type _firing area_ (see the
  link:../admin-manual/manual.html#areas[Areas] chapter of the administrator manual).
* A domain of its own, such as _Firing Areas_, holds a published message for each firing area,
  with the firing area among its areas, which describes the area: its limits, the signals, whom to
  contact, what is prohibited. Editors keep these _firing area messages_ up to date in the editor,
  like any other message.
* That domain has a firing schedule, and with it the _Schedule_ page this chapter describes, where
  editors enter the firing periods.
* The firing schedule names the domain and the message series that receive the firing exercise
  messages, such as a domain _Firing Exercises_, and how many days ahead they cover.

[#firing-schedule-page]
=== The Schedule Page

To open the page, choose the domain of the firing areas in the domain menu of the header, and click
*Schedule* in the main menu. *Schedule* is in the menu when you have the editor, administrator or
system administrator role and the current domain has a firing schedule. In another domain the page
says "Domain has no firing schedule".

image::FiringAreaSchedule.png[The Firing Area Schedule page, 800]

The page shows the firing areas of the domain on one day:

* *Area name*: type a part of a name to list only the areas whose name holds it.
* *Select areas*: the pin opens the areas, where you tick the parts of the waters to list, such as
  _Kattegat_; each one chosen shows beside the pin, and a click on it takes it out again. The
  cross at the right clears the choice.
* *Schedule Date*: the day shown. The page opens on today; pick another day in the calendar, or
  step a day back or forward with the arrows (*Previous day*, *Next day*).
* The table lists the firing areas under the waters they belong to. *Time* shows each area's
  firing periods on that day, such as "7:00 AM - 9:00 PM CET", and the pencil beside them edits
  them. An area without firing periods that day has an empty *Time*; when no area matches, the page
  says "No firing areas for this date".
* The map shows the firing areas of the table. Point at a row of the table, and its area is marked
  on the map. *Layers* switches the areas and the base map on and off.

Times are in the time zone of the domain, and its abbreviation (such as CET) follows them.

*Update Firing Exercises…*, at the top, updates the firing exercise messages at once
(<<firing-exercises-generation>>).

[#firing-periods]
=== Adding and Editing Firing Periods

To change an area's firing periods on the day shown, click the pencil (*Edit firing periods*) in
its row. The row opens a line for each period, with its start and end time.

image::FiringAreaScheduleEdit.png[An area's firing periods being edited, 460]

* Each line has two time fields, marked with a clock: the start of the period on the left and its
  end on the right. Click a field and pick the time: the hour, the minutes, and AM or PM.
* *Add* adds a line for another period, and the trash icon at the end of a line takes that line
  out.
* *Clear* takes out all the lines, except a period that started on an earlier day (see below).
* *Save* stores the lines as the area's firing periods, for that area and that day only, and closes
  the row. Nothing is stored before you click *Save*: *Clear* followed by *Save* deletes all the
  periods of the day. *Cancel* closes the row and leaves the periods as they were; when you have
  added, taken out or changed a line, Niord first asks whether to leave without saving.

A firing period is entered on the day it starts. A period without a start time starts at midnight,
and one without an end time lasts until the end of the day; a line with neither is left out. To fire until
midnight, enter 00:00 (12:00 AM) as the end: the period then lasts until midnight at the end of the
day shown, and the firing exercise message reads, for example, "hours 09:00 - 24:00". For an
exercise that goes on past midnight, enter one period until 00:00 and another from 00:00 on the
next day.

A period must end after it starts. When the end of a line is before its start, or the same, the
line says "The period must end after it starts. To fire until midnight, enter 00:00 as the end."
and *Save* cannot be clicked until the line is corrected.

An older period can be stored with its end before its start, because Niord did not check this
earlier (for example 09:00 to 00:00 of the same day, or 08:00 to 23:00 of the day before). Such a
period has a warning mark beside it in the day's list;
pointing at the mark says what is wrong. Its time fields can look right ("8:00 AM - 11:00 PM"), so
when you open the row, the line under it names the end it was saved with, for example "This period
was saved with its end at 24 Nov 2026, 11:00 PM CET, before its start. Choose the end time again to
correct it." Pick the end time again (the same time, 00:00, or the end it should have) and save:
the end is then stored on the day shown. An end of 00:00 is stored as midnight at the end of that
day.

Such an older period that was meant to go on past midnight, for example one stored as 22:00 to
02:00 of 24 Nov, is also shown on the day after it starts, for its early hours. Its dates are shown
on both ends ("24 Nov 2026, 10:00 PM - 24 Nov 2026, 2:00 AM CET"), so it does not read as that
night's hours. On the later day it cannot be changed, as any period of an earlier day (see below):
correct it on the day it starts. A period stored as 09:00 to 00:00 of the same day, or 08:00 to
23:00 of the day before, is shown on its start day only.

A period that started on the day before and goes on past midnight (made, for example, by another
system) is also shown on the later day. There it cannot be changed: it shows as text with "Starts
on an earlier day. Edit it on that day.", *Clear* leaves it, and *Save* keeps it as it is. On the
day it starts you can change its start time, and it keeps its end on the next day. A changed end
time is read on the day shown, so an end before the start makes the line say "The period must end
after it starts. It runs past midnight: to keep firing after midnight, end it at 00:00 and add a
period from 00:00 on the next day." Times and dates are shown on both ends of such a period, and of
a period until midnight. A period you do not change is saved as it was. When Niord cannot store the periods it says "Error updating firing
periods".

The times are read in the time zone of the domain you are working in. When the domain has no time
zone, the pencil cannot be used ("No time zone — choose a domain first").

[#firing-exercises-generation]
=== Firing Exercise Messages

Niord makes the firing exercise messages from the firing area messages and the schedule. For each
firing area with periods from today up to the number of days the firing schedule covers (30 days
unless the system administrator has set another number), it publishes a message in the domain and
message series of the firing schedule:

* a local warning (a temporary notice, when the series holds notices to mariners), already
  _Published_;
* with the firing area message's text, areas, categories, charts and positions;
* with a first part that lists the firing periods, as text in the time zone of the receiving
  domain and as the message's event dates;
* with the subject "Firing exercises. Warning." in English (the wording comes from the system's
  dictionary);
* published from the moment it is made until the end of its last period.

Each time it runs, Niord compares the messages it would make with those already published:

* an area whose periods are unchanged keeps its message;
* an area whose periods have changed gets a new message, and the old one is cancelled;
* an area without periods any more has its message cancelled.

A change to a firing area message alone, with the same periods, does not change a firing exercise
message already published; it reaches the firing exercises with the next change of that area's
periods.

Niord does this by itself once an hour. *Update Firing Exercises…* on the _Schedule_ page does it
at once, for every active firing schedule: Niord asks first ("Firing exercises are automatically
updated from the schedule every hour. Do you wish to update them now?"), and *Yes* updates the
messages ("Firing Exercises have been updated"), *No* does nothing.

image::FiringAreaScheduleUpdate.png[The question before the firing exercises are updated, 280]

The firing exercise messages are made by Niord, so you do not edit them: change the schedule, or
the firing area message, instead. You find them on the *Messages* page of the receiving domain,
such as _Firing Exercises_ (see <<messages>>), and, when that domain is published, on the public
site and through the public interfaces.

An administrator can create a draft firing area message for every firing area at once: *Generate
firing area messages…* in the action menu (the cog) of the *Areas* page. The action is there only
in a domain that has a firing schedule, such as the domain of the firing areas (see the
link:../admin-manual/manual.html#areas[Areas] chapter of the administrator manual). Editors then
complete the drafts and publish them.

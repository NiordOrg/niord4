[#publications]
== Publications

Niord keeps the publications that go out alongside the messages: the weekly Notices to Mariners, a
list of the temporary and preliminary notices in force, a yearly compilation, an uploaded handbook,
a link to a list of lights. Some of them Niord makes itself from the messages; others are files or
web addresses that you add. A publication can be cited from a message where its settings allow it
(see <<publication-series-basics>>), and a publication in a public category is offered for download
on the public site.

You manage publications on the _Publications_ page of the _Admin_ area: open _Admin_ in the main
menu, then _Publications_ in the menu on the left. The page needs the administrator role. Each
publication belongs to one domain (see <<publication-domains>>), so the page lists the publications
of the domain you have chosen in the header; choose another domain there to work on its
publications.

The categories that group the publications on the public site have a page of their own, described
in the next chapter, <<publication-categories>>.

[#publication-concepts]
=== Series, Issues and One-off Publications

Four words describe every publication in Niord:

[cols="25,75",options="header"]
|===
| Term
| What it is

| Series
| A publication that comes out again and again, for example the weekly Notices to Mariners. The
  series holds the settings that all its issues follow: its category, what its content is, how
  often it comes out, how its issues and files are named and, for a series that Niord makes from
  the messages, which messages it selects.

| Issue
| One released document of a series, for example the Notices to Mariners of week 36. An issue is
  _Open_ while it is being prepared, _Published_ once it has gone out, and _Withdrawn_ if it was
  taken off the public list afterwards. Every issue has a permanent link of its own.

| One-off publication
| A publication that comes out once: an uploaded document, a link to a document elsewhere, or a
  publication that only exists so that messages can cite it (a list of wrecks, a journal number).
  A one-off publication is a series with exactly one issue.

| Category
| A group of publications, such as "Notices to Mariners" or "External publications". The category
  decides whether its publications are shown on the public site, and in which order.
|===

An issue carries four moments, and it helps to keep them apart:

[cols="25,75",options="header"]
|===
| Moment
| What it means

| Content period
| The stretch of time whose messages the issue carries, from its _Period start_ to its cut-off.
  The next issue's content period starts where this one's ended.

| Cut-off
| The instant at which it is decided which messages are in the issue. While the issue is open, its
  _Planned cut-off_ is only a plan; the cut-off is stamped when you publish.

| Published
| When somebody pressed _Publish_ and the issue went out.

| Public period
| When the public site lists the issue. It starts when the issue is published and normally ends
  when the next issue of the series is published.
|===

How often a series comes out and how it selects its messages are two separate settings. A weekly
Notices to Mariners and a weekly list of the notices in force both come out every week, but the
first carries the messages _published_ during the week, while the second carries every message
that is still _in force_ at the cut-off, however old it is (see <<publication-criteria>>).

[#publication-roles]
=== Who May Do What

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Role
| What it allows

| Administrator
| Everything in this chapter and the next: creating and changing series, one-off publications and
  categories, preparing, publishing, amending and withdrawing issues, and adding or leaving out
  messages by hand; no other role allows adding or leaving out messages. An administrator changes
  the publications of the domains he or she administers; a publication of another domain can be
  read but not changed (see <<publication-domains>>).

| Editor
| Cites published issues in a message, through the _Publications_ field of the message editor, and
  sees which issues a message has gone out in (see
  link:../editor-manual/manual.html#publication-editor-field[the Publications field] in the editor
  manual).

| System administrator
| Manages the print reports that render the documents of the generated series (see
  link:../sysadmin-manual/manual.html#print-reports[Print Reports] in the system administrator
  manual), besides everything an administrator may do.
|===

[#publications-page]
=== The Publications Page

image::PublicationsOverview.png[Publications, 800]

The page shows the publications of the current domain in three groups:

* *The series cards.* One card for each active or draft series, three to a row.
* *One-offs.* A table of the one-off publications.
* *Retired.* The series that are no longer used, folded away under a line with their count. Click
  the line to show them; click a name to open the series.

Above the groups:

* _Filter publications…_ narrows all three groups to the publications whose name holds the text
  you type. The filter is kept in the page's address, so a filtered list survives a reload and can
  be sent as a link.
* _New_ creates a series or a one-off publication (_New series_, _New one-off publication_).
* The cog menu _Publication actions_ exports and imports series definitions (see
  <<publication-import-export>>).

[#publications-page-cards]
==== The Series Card

image::PublicationSeriesCard.png[A Series Card, 211]

A card tells you at a glance whether a series is keeping up:

* The name, which opens the series page, and how often the series comes out (_Weekly_, _Yearly_,
  _No fixed cadence_). A _Draft_ badge marks a series that does not publish yet. A retired series
  has no card: it is listed in the _Retired_ group.
* A row of numbered cells: one cell for each of the last periods, numbered by week, month or year. A
  filled cell is an open issue, an outlined one an issue that went out, a dashed one a period for
  which nothing was released, and a quiet one a period still to come. Point at a cell to read its
  status and number of messages; click it to open that issue, or, for a missing period, the series
  page where the period can be created. A line under the cells counts the missing periods.
* _Open: <name>_ is the issue being prepared, with its expected _Cut-off_ and the number of
  messages it holds so far. The cut-off is shown in italics with a clock, because it is only
  stamped when somebody publishes.
* _Open the issue_ goes straight to the open issue; _All issues_ opens the series page. A series
  with no open issue offers _New issue_ instead (see <<publication-new-issue>>).

A series with no fixed frequency has no periods to draw. Its card says how many issues it has and
which came out last, and what kind of content its next issue would be (_Issues are uploaded as
PDF_, _Issues are external links_). A series that has released nothing for several periods is
marked _dormant_.

[#publications-page-one-offs]
==== The One-offs Table

image::PublicationOneOffTable.png[One-off Publications, 640]

The table lists the one-off publications of the domain with their _Type_ (_Uploaded PDF_,
_External link_ or _Reference only_), their _Status_ (_Draft_, _Published_ or _Withdrawn_), when
they are _Public_ on the public site, from which domains they _Can be cited from_ and the _File /
link_ behind them, with a button that copies its address. Click the _Public_ heading to sort the
rows by the start of their public period, again to reverse the order, and a third time for the
original order.

Click a name to open the publication's own page (see <<publication-one-offs>>). The cog at the end
of a row offers _Edit_, _Move to domain…_ (where you administer another domain to move it to)
and, for a published publication, _Withdraw_.

When the domain has no one-off publications the table says so, and reminds you that publications
owned by another domain can still be cited from messages here.

[#publication-series-settings]
=== Creating and Configuring a Series

[[publication-templates-tab]]
[[adding-or-editing-publication-templates]]To create a series, click _New_ and choose _New series_. To change one, open the series and click
_Settings_. Both open the _Series settings_ drawer, which has four tabs: _Basics_, _Content_,
_Frequency & naming_ and _Criteria_. A new series is created as a draft in the current domain and
publishes nothing until you activate it (see <<publication-series-lifecycle>>).

To start from an existing series, open it and choose _Copy series_ in its cog menu: the drawer
opens as a new series with every setting of the original filled in, and a new _Series ID_ to give.

[#publication-series-basics]
==== Basics

[[message-publication]]
image::PublicationSeriesBasics.png[Series Settings: Basics, 512]

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Field
| Description

| Name
| The name of the series, one field per language.

| Series ID
| The permanent identity of the series. Niord proposes one from the name; you can change it until
  the series is created, and never afterwards, because imports, exports and every stored citation
  use it.

| Languages
| The languages the series is written in. Each language gets its own name, patterns and, if
  wanted, its own file.

| Category
| The publication category the series belongs to. The category's _Show on the public site_ setting
  decides whether the published issues appear on the public site.

| Owner domain
| The domain the series belongs to: the current domain when it was created. It is shown, not
  chosen; see <<publication-domains>> for moving a series.

| [[none-file-type]][[link-file-type]][[uploaded-file-file-type]]Content type
| What an issue holds: _Generated from criteria_ (Niord selects the messages by the criteria on the
  _Criteria_ tab and renders a document at every release), _Uploaded PDF_ (a file you upload for
  each issue), _External link_ (an address of a document elsewhere) or _Reference only_ (no
  document; the publication exists to be cited).

| Can be cited from messages in
| From which domains a message may cite the series' issues: _This domain only_, _Selected domains_
  (tick the domains in the list that appears) or _Every domain_. Only the owner domain can change
  the series.

| Selectable as a reference in messages
| Whether editors can cite the series in a message, and in which list of the message: _No_ (the
  series cannot be cited at all), _Internal_, or _External (shown publicly on the message)_. It can
  be changed only while the series
  is a draft: citations already written live in the field that was chosen at the time.

| Reference format
| Shown when the series can be cited: how the citation reads on a message, one per language, for
  example `NtM ${week}/${year}`. The token `${parameters}` stands for what the editor types when
  citing, such as a light number.
|===

[#publication-series-content]
==== Content

image::PublicationSeriesContent.png[Series Settings: Content, 502]

The fields of the report are shown for a series generated from criteria; a series of uploads or
links has only the file and link patterns.

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Field
| Description

| Report template
| The print report that renders the issue's document, chosen from the reports a system
  administrator keeps (see link:../sysadmin-manual/manual.html#print-reports[Print Reports]). A
  generated series cannot be activated without one.

| Page size, Orientation, Map thumbnails
| How the report is printed.

| Report parameters
| Values handed to the report, as name and value pairs (_add parameter_). The values the issue
  supplies, `week`, `weekTo`, `weeks`, `year`, `edition`, `yearNumber` and `weekNumber`, are set
  from the issue and cannot be typed here, nor for one issue under _Tailor issue_. Where the report
  names parameters of its own, they are offered to add.

| Sort messages by, Sort direction
| The order of the messages in the document: by _Areas_, _Publish date_, _Event dates_,
  _Follow-up date_ or _ID_, _Ascending_ or _Descending_. _The domain's sort order_ leaves the order
  to the publication's domain, and to areas, ascending, when the domain sets none.

| A separate file per language
| Whether each language gets its own document, or one document serves every language.

| File name pattern
| How the document's file is named, one per language, for example `NtM-Week-${week}-${year}.pdf`.
  Two languages cannot share one file name.

| Link pattern
| For a series of external links: how the address of each issue is built.
|===

The patterns can use these tokens, which the _Insert_ button beside each field also offers:
`${week}`, `${week-2-digits}`, `${weekTo}`, `${weekTo-2-digits}`, `${year}`, `${year-2-digits}`,
`${month}`, `${month-2-digits}`, `${day}`, `${day-2-digits}` and `${edition}`. They are filled in
from the issue's cut-off: an issue is named after the week, month and year in which its content
period ends, read in the time zone of the series' domain. `${weekTo}` is the closing week of an
issue that covers more than one week, such as a double issue over a holiday; for a single week
it is left out together with the text that joins it to `${week}` (see below). A pattern writes
the two weeks as `${week}+${weekTo}`: a double issue then reads "Week 51+52", and an issue of
three weeks or more reads as a range, "Week 36-38" (the `+` between the two tokens prints as a
hyphen). A single week under such a pattern leaves `${weekTo}` out together with the
text between it and `${week}`: "Week ${week}+${weekTo}" names it "Week 36", and "Week ${week} and
${weekTo}" does too, in the name, the file name and the reference format alike. A bracket around
the closing week goes with it ("Week ${week} (${weekTo})" is "Week 36"), and a `${weekTo}` with
no `${week}` beside it prints the single week ("Week ${weekTo}" is "Week 36"). So a pattern that
writes both weeks serves a series whose issues are double only now and then. A published issue
keeps the name and file name it was given; an open issue that follows the series gets the new
name when its numbers are next worked out.

The weekly reports print the weeks in their heading as the series' name pattern writes them when
the document is generated: under `${week}+${weekTo}` a double issue is headed "Week 51+52" and a
single week "Week 36"; under a pattern that writes the first week alone, as the series carried
over from the previous system do, a double issue is headed by its first week. A name typed for one
issue does not change the heading; a week written under _Numbering_ does, and is printed as
written. The values can be overridden for one issue (see <<publication-tailor>>).

[#publication-series-frequency]
==== Frequency & Naming

image::PublicationSeriesFrequency.png[Series Settings: Frequency & Naming, 502]

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Field
| Description

| Frequency
| _No fixed cadence_, _Daily_, _Weekly_, _Monthly_ or _Yearly_.

| Nominal cut-off time, Cut-off weekday, Cut-off month, Cut-off day of month
| The planned schedule: the time of day and, depending on the frequency, the weekday, the day of
  the month and the month on which a period normally closes. The actual cut-off is stamped when an
  issue is published, and the next issue starts exactly there.

| Release
| How an issue goes out. _Manual gate_: the issue is prepared automatically, but only goes out
  when somebody clicks _Publish_. This is the only way issues are released.

| Cut-off falls at
| Where the content period ends by default: at _The release moment_, or, for a yearly series only,
  at _The start of the period_ or _The end of the period_. When publishing you can still choose
  the planned cut-off instead of the present moment.

| Numbering
| How the issues are numbered: _Not numbered_, _Week/year (ISO week)_, _Year and edition_,
  _Month/year_ or _Running edition number_.

| New issue is created
| Shown, not chosen. A series with a frequency opens its next issue itself as soon as the current
  one is published (_Automatically when the previous one publishes_); a series without one has
  each issue created by hand.

| Name suggestion pattern
| How a new issue is named, one per language, for example `NtM Week ${week} - ${year}`. The same
  tokens as above can be used.

| First interval starts
| For a series that selects messages published in an interval: where the very first content
  period opens. Later issues follow on from the previous cut-off.
|===

[#publication-criteria]
=== Criteria and Time Relation

[[message-file-type]]
image::PublicationSeriesCriteria.png[Series Settings: Criteria, 502]

The _Criteria_ tab decides which messages a series generated from criteria puts in each issue. It
has two parts.

*Time relation* says how a message's dates place it in an issue:

[cols="35,65",options="header"]
|===
| Time relation
| Which messages an issue holds

| Published in the issue's interval
| Every message published between the previous cut-off and this one. Use it for a publication of
  what is new, such as the weekly Notices to Mariners.

| In force at the cut-off
| Every message that is still in force at the cut-off, whenever it was published. Use it for a
  list of what applies now, such as the temporary and preliminary notices in force.

| Compiled from another series
| Everything the issues of another series printed during the period. Use it for a yearly
  compilation (see <<publication-compilations>>).
|===

_Only messages still in force at the cut-off_ leaves out the messages that were cancelled or had
expired by the cut-off. For _In force at the cut-off_ it is always on.

*The criteria* say which messages qualify at all. Add a criterion with the buttons after _Add
criterion:_ (_Message series_, _Main type_, _Message type_, _Domain_, _Area_, _Category_, _Chart_),
then tick or pick its values. Every criterion must match: they are combined with AND, so a
_Message series_ criterion with the series of your notices to mariners and a _Message type_
criterion with the temporary and preliminary notices select the temporary and preliminary notices
of that message series. Within one criterion, any of the ticked values matches. Each kind of criterion can be used once, a series can
hold at most eight criteria, and every series needs at least one message series or domain, so that
it does not reach every message in the installation. Remove a criterion with its bin.
An _Area_ or _Category_ criterion holds the MRN of each area or category, and that MRN is matched
without regard to case: one given in other capital letters in an imported file selects the same
messages, and so does a series whose area or category later has its MRN re-typed in other
capital letters.

_What this would select_ tries the criteria on the messages as you change them and shows how many
messages they would select now, with a sample of their numbers and any warnings, for example
messages that would also belong to another issue.

Where the old Niord used a free "message tag filter" expression and a message tag for each issue,
the criteria and the time relation now do the work: an in-force list is _In force at the cut-off_
with the criteria for the message types, and a weekly list of everything published in the week is
_Published in the issue's interval_.

[#publication-series-lifecycle]
=== Draft, Active and Retired Series

A series is in one of three states:

* *Draft*: being set up. It accepts no issues and editors cannot cite it.
* *Active*: in use. It accepts issues and appears in the message editor's list of publications.
* *Retired*: no longer used for new issues. The issues it has published stay on the public list,
  and their files stay at their links.

The state is changed from the cog menu _Publication actions_ on the series page, which also holds
_Copy series_ and _Move to domain…_:

* _Activate_ checks the series' rules once more and activates it. A draft series also offers
  _Activate the series…_ in the note at the top of its page.
* _Retire series_ asks for a reason, which is kept in the series' history.
* _Reinstate series_ makes a retired series active again, after checking its rules.

[#publication-check-rules]
==== Checking the Rules

image::PublicationSeriesCheckRules.png[Checking a New Series, 512]

Before saving, click _Check rules_ at the bottom of the settings drawer. Niord checks the series as
it stands in the drawer, without saving it, and either says _Every rule passes. The series can be
activated._ or lists each rule that fails, under the name of the field it concerns, for example
that a series generated from criteria needs a report and at least one criterion. The same rules
are checked when you save and when you activate the series, and a field that breaks one is marked
in place.

[#publication-series-page]
=== The Series Page

[[publications-tab]]
image::PublicationSeriesPage.png[The Series Page, 800]

Click a series' name on the _Publications_ page to open its page:

* *The header* names the series, how often it comes out and what is _Public now_ on the public site:
  the current issue and its public period. _Settings_ opens the settings drawer, and the cog menu
  _Publication actions_ holds _Copy series_, _Move to domain…_ and _Activate_, _Retire series_
  or _Reinstate series_.
* *Current issue* shows the open issue with its planned cut-off, the number of messages and how far
  it is from being published (_Ready to publish_, or the number of warnings), and _Open the issue_.
  When nothing is open, it says _No open issue_ and offers _New issue_.
* *The issue list* holds every issue of the series, newest first, 20 to a page, with its _Status_,
  _Cut-off_, _Public period_ and number of _Messages_. _Filter issues…_ narrows the list by name.
  Click _Issue_ or _Cut-off_ to sort by that column. A series numbered by edition (_Year and
  edition_, _Running edition number_) also has an _Edition_ column, which sorts the same way. Click
  a row to open the issue.
* *History of the series*, folded away at the bottom, records what was done to the series itself:
  its activation and retirement, a move to another domain, and the one line that is left when an
  issue is deleted.

image::PublicationSeriesHistory.png[History of the Series, 640]

A cut-off that was not stamped when its issue was published is marked with how it was found:
_reconstructed_ for an issue carried over from the previous system, _chosen_ for a cut-off picked
by hand when publishing. A cut-off or public period in italics with a clock is still expected, not
recorded.

For a series with a frequency, Niord also looks for periods with no issue. A period for which
nothing was released shows as a _Missing_ row, and the period being worked towards as an
_Upcoming_ row. On an active series of your domain, a _Missing_ row has a _Create_ button that
opens the new issue dialog for that period. An _Upcoming_ row has it only while no issue is open,
because the series opens that period itself when the current issue is published. When
the periods were not looked for, a line above the list says why: for example, that the series is
dormant because nothing has been released for several periods. A series that has no issue at all
says _No issues yet_ instead of the list, with no rows for its periods: its card on the
_Publications_ page shows the missing periods, and _New issue_ under _Current issue_ opens the
dialog for the first of them.

[#publication-new-issue]
=== Creating an Issue

[[adding-or-editing-publication]]
A series with a frequency opens its next issue by itself as soon as the current one is published,
so you rarely create an issue by hand. You do so for a series without a fixed frequency, for a
period that was missed, or when no issue is open: click _New issue_ on the series card or the
series page, or _Create_ on a _Missing_ or _Upcoming_ row.

image::PublicationNewIssueDialog.png[New Issue, 400]

The _New issue_ dialog shows the issue before it is created: its _Period start_ and _Period end_
(change them if the proposal is wrong), each with a word that says where the proposal came from,
such as _nominal_ for a moment taken from the series' schedule; its name in each language; and its
edition, when it is not the first. _Would be selected_ says how many messages the period would
hold (_Not counted_ when it cannot be counted, for example for a series that is not generated from
criteria or a period with no end yet), and _Chained from_ names
the issue whose cut-off the period follows on from (a series' first issue has none). Warnings tell you what to know before you
create it, for example that the period does not start where the previous issue closed, or, for a
series that lists what is in force at the cut-off, that the period has no start. Click _Create
issue_ to create it.

[#publication-issue]
=== Working on an Open Issue

image::PublicationOpenIssue.png[An Open Issue, 800]

An open issue is the one being prepared. Open it with _Open the issue_ on the series card or the
series page. Its page shows:

* *The header*: the issue's name and status, the series, and how it is released. _Tailor issue_,
  _Edit_ and the cog menu _Publication actions_ are on the right.
* *The facts*: the _Content period_ (with how its start was found), the _Planned cut-off_, the
  _Public period_ (empty until the issue is published) and the _Criteria_ in use.
* *Messages in this issue*: the messages the issue holds, with their _Status_, the date they were
  _Published_ and _Why_ they are in the issue (_Published in the period_, _Still in force at the
  cut-off_, _Printed by its source issue_, _Added by hand_).
* *Left out by the criteria*: the messages near the issue that the criteria leave out, each with
  the reason.
* On the right, the *Checklist* with the _Publish…_ button (see <<publication-publish>>), the
  *Document* and the *History* of the issue.

[#publication-issue-view]
==== As of Now or As of the Planned Cut-off

An open issue is a moving target: messages are published, cancelled and corrected while you
prepare it. The two buttons above the list show the issue _As of now_ (what would go out if you
published at this moment) or _As of the planned cut-off_. Messages published after the planned
cut-off are gathered under _Published after the planned cut-off_, marked _Included if you publish
now_ or _Not included at the planned cut-off_, so you can see what your choice of cut-off decides.

[#publication-issue-left-out]
==== Messages Left Out by the Criteria

image::PublicationIssueLeftOut.png[Left Out by the Criteria, 401]

Click _Left out by the criteria_ to see the messages that the criteria considered and rejected,
each with the reason: _Published before the period started_, _Published after the cut-off_, _Not
in force at the cut-off_, _Not published_, _Does not match the criteria_ or _No publication date_.
When the list is long, it shows the most recent and says so. Click a message to open it. A message
left out wrongly can be added by hand (see <<publication-curation>>).

[#publication-issue-edit]
==== Editing the Period and Edition

Click _Edit_ to change the issue's _Edition_ (usually 1; raise it when the same period goes out
more than once), its _Period start_ and its _Planned cut-off_. The issue's name is changed with
_Tailor issue_ (see <<publication-tailor>>), together with its file name and numbering. Moving the
start changes which messages fall in the period; moving the planned cut-off changes the name the
series suggests and what the planned view shows. What actually goes out is decided by the cut-off
you confirm when you publish.

In the same editor you can tailor the _Criteria_ for this issue alone, for example to leave out an
area for one week. The issue then shows its own criteria; _Use the series criteria again_ goes
back to the series' ones. The pencil beside the criteria in the facts opens the same editor. _Save_
keeps the changes; _Cancel_, or _Cancel edit_ in the header, throws them away.

[#publication-tailor]
==== Tailoring an Issue

image::PublicationTailorIssue.png[Tailor Issue, 512]

_Tailor issue_ opens the _Tailor this issue_ drawer, for the few things that have to differ from
the series for one issue:

* _Name_ and _File name_ in each language. Under each field Niord says what the series would
  suggest.
* _Numbering_: the _Week_, _Year_ and _Edition_ printed on the issue. They are worked out from the
  cut-off, but are free text. Under a name pattern that writes the first week alone (`${week}`),
  write "51 & 52" for a double issue, and the name, the file name and the report's heading follow.
  A pattern that also writes `${weekTo}` adds its own closing week after what you write
  ("Week 51 & 52+52"), so leave the week as it is worked out there.
* _Report_ and _Report parameters_: another report, or other parameter values, for this issue only.

A tailored field shows _Follow the series again_ beside it, and _Reset all tailoring_ undoes them
all. A tailored issue is marked _Tailored_ beside its name.

[#publication-issue-document]
==== The Document

image::PublicationIssueDocument.png[The Document Card, 223]

The _Document_ card shows the issue's file in each language and where it came from:

* For a series generated from criteria the document is _generated when you publish_. _Generate
  preview_ renders it now, as it would look if you published at the moment the list is shown at
  (_As of now_ or _As of the planned cut-off_), and _Download preview_ opens it. _Replace document…_
  lets you upload a file of your own instead; an uploaded file is kept as it is, and publishing
  does not generate over it.
* For a series of uploaded files, _Upload file…_ adds the file, and _Clear file_ removes it
  again.
* For a series of external links, type the address in _External link_ and click _Save link_.

[#publication-curation]
=== Adding and Excluding Messages by Hand

The criteria decide which messages an issue holds, but you can overrule them for one issue. Every
such decision needs a reason of 3 to 512 characters, is shown beside the message in the issue's
list and is kept in the issue's history with your name and the time. Only an open issue can be
changed this way.

To add a message that the criteria leave out, click _Add message_ above the list. In the dialog,
search the message by its number or title (only published, expired and cancelled messages of the
domain can be added), choose it, type the reason and click _Add to the issue_.

image::PublicationAddMessageDialog.png[Add a Message by Hand, 400]

To take messages out, tick them in _Messages in this issue_ and click _Exclude selected_, or click
_Exclude_ at the end of one row. Type the reason and click _Exclude_. The messages stay published;
only what this issue contains changes.

image::PublicationExcludeDialog.png[Exclude Messages, 320]

A message added by hand shows _Added by hand_ and who added it; an excluded one stays in the list,
marked as left out by you, with _Include again_. _Withdraw decision_ removes a manual decision
altogether, and the criteria decide again.

[#publication-publish]
=== The Checklist and Publishing

[[releasing-publications]]
image::PublicationChecklist.png[The Checklist, 223]

The _Checklist_ on the right of an open issue checks whether the issue can go out, and is checked
again whenever the issue changes. Its verdict is _Ready to publish_, a number of warnings that do
not stop you (_2 warnings — can be published_), or a number of problems _stopping the release_.
Each warning and problem is explained; click the line with the number of checks passed to see the
others. The checks:

[cols="45,55",options="header"]
|===
| Check
| What it looks at

| The issue is open and its series is active
| Only an open issue of an active series can be published.

| The content period has a start
| A series that selects by interval needs a period start.

| The period starts where the previous issue ended
| A gap or an overlap with the previous issue.

| The cut-off is not in the future, is after the previous issue's, is before the next issue's
| A content period cannot end in the future, reopen a closed period or take content that has
  already gone out.

| No other issue covers this period
| Two issues of the same series sharing messages.

| The messages have been resolved; the number of messages is within the limit
| The issue's messages could be worked out, and there are no more than 1,000 of them.

| No message cancelled after the cut-off is still counted
| A message cancelled after the cut-off that is still dated as in force at it.

| Every manual include or exclude still applies
| A decision made by hand that no longer concerns any message.

| Every language has a file; every language has a citation format; a report is configured
| What the document and the citations need.

| Every period of the source series has a published issue
| For a compilation: a source period with nothing published, or a source issue still open.
|===

A check that does not concern the issue, such as a report for a series of uploads, is left out:
it is not shown, and it is not counted in the number of checks passed or in the verdict. That is
why two issues can show a different number of checks passed.

To publish, click _Publish…_. The dialog asks for the cut-off:

image::PublicationPublishDialog.png[Publish, 400]

* _At the planned cut-off_ ends the content period at the planned moment. _Change…_ moves the
  planned cut-off first.
* _Now_ ends the content period at the present moment.

The sentence at the top says what will happen: how many messages go out, when the cut-off is
stamped, that the document is generated and, for a series with a frequency, that the next issue
opens. If the checklist has warnings, they are listed and you acknowledge each before _Publish_ can
be pressed.

Publishing an issue:

. stamps the cut-off and freezes the list of messages: later changes to a message no longer move
  it in or out of the issue;
. generates the document from the series' report (unless a file was uploaded or linked);
. opens the issue's public period, and ends the public period of the issue it follows;
. opens the next issue of a series with a frequency.

[#publication-after-publish]
=== After Publishing: Amend, New Edition, Withdraw and Delete

image::PublicationPublishedIssue.png[A Published Issue, 800]

A published issue shows the messages that went out, marked _frozen at publication_. A message that
has changed since then, for example expired, been cancelled or changed its type, keeps its place in
the issue and gets a warning sign; point at it to read what changed. Amending the issue runs the
selection again, so the issue in the picture, which was amended once, has no such sign. The
_Checklist_ card now
shows when the issue was published, its public period and its number of messages, and the
issue's _History_ keeps the checklist as it was at publication.

For a series that selects its messages by criteria, a message published after the issue, with a
publish date inside the issue's period, belongs to that period and so to no other issue of the
series. The published issue then shows a warning that
names these messages, with _Amend_ to take them in; the message's _Publications_ panel in the
editor marks the issue _Missed by a released issue_.

image::PublicationIssueHistory.png[Issue History, 223]

The header offers _Copy link_ (the issue's permanent address) and _Withdraw_, and the cog menu
_Publication actions_ the other actions:

* *Amend* runs the selection again against the same cut-off and replaces the document where it
  stands, with the same links, the same public period and the same cut-off. Use it when something
  has to be corrected: messages cancelled, retyped or corrected since, or a change to the series'
  criteria or report. The messages are put in order again by the series' sort as it stands now,
  so a change to the sort since the release shows in the amended document. Amending asks for a
  reason; the replaced document is archived and can still
  be opened from the issue's history (_Replaced document_). An issue carried over from the
  previous system cannot be amended: its messages and document are a historical record.
+
image::PublicationAmendDialog.png[Amend Issue, 340]
* *New edition* creates a new, open issue for the same period, linked to this one. This issue stays
  the current one on the public site until the new edition is published. It is not offered on a
  series that opens its next issue by itself, nor on a one-off publication.
* *Withdraw* takes the issue off the public list, with a reason. Its file and links stay, so the
  messages that cite it keep working. A withdrawn issue offers *Reinstate*, which puts it back on
  the public list with the public period it had.
* *Delete issue…* removes an open or withdrawn issue for good, with its history. A published issue
  must be withdrawn first, and an issue that a message still cites cannot be deleted: Niord names
  the citing messages instead. The series keeps a one-line record of the deletion.

[#publication-compilations]
=== Compilations

A compilation collects what the issues of another series printed. A yearly accumulated Notices to
Mariners, for example, is a series with the time relation _Compiled from another series_ and the
weekly Notices to Mariners as its _Source series_:

image::PublicationSeriesCompiledFrom.png[A Compiled Series, 502]

A compilation has no criteria of its own: its messages are the frozen lists of the source series'
published issues. Only a series generated from criteria can be a source, and a compilation cannot
compile another compilation.

image::PublicationCompilationOpen.png[An Open Compilation, 616]

A compilation lists its messages grouped by the source issue they came from, with the week and
the period of each; click a group to show its messages. Messages added by hand follow the source
issues' groups in a group of their own, _Added by hand_: the picture shows the end of the list of
an open compilation, the last source groups folded and the _Added by hand_ group opened. While the
compilation is open, the _Source issues_ card on the right lists the source issues its period
holds, and the checklist warns when a period of the source series has no published issue or a
source issue is still open.

Once published, the compilation keeps the groups as they were at its cut-off:

image::PublicationCompilationPublished.png[A Published Compilation, 800]

The document of a compilation is rendered by its own report, which a system administrator sets up
in the print reports.

[#publication-one-offs]
=== One-off Publications

To add a one-off publication, click _New_ on the _Publications_ page and choose _New one-off
publication_.

image::PublicationNewOneOffDialog.png[New One-off Publication, 512]

Fill in:

* _Name_, in each language.
* _Type_: _Reference only_, _Uploaded PDF_ or _External link_ (type the address in each language).
* _Category_ and _Can be cited from messages in_, as for a series. The _Owner domain_ is the
  current domain.
* Under _Advanced_, if needed: the _Identifier_ (derived from the name when left empty; it cannot
  be changed later), the _Sort order_, _A separate file per language_, the _File name pattern_ and
  whether and how messages can cite the publication (_Selectable as a reference in messages_,
  _Reference format_).

_Check rules_ checks the publication without saving it, as for a series. Click _Create as draft_.
The file of an uploaded publication is added after this first save, on the publication's own
page, which opens when you click its name in the one-offs table:

image::PublicationOneOffPage.png[A One-off Publication, 800]

* *Document* shows the file or the link, with _Upload file…_ or _Replace file…_, and the
  citation a message will show.
* *On the public site* says whether, and when, the publication is listed on the public site, with
  its status, its public period and its category. _Publish_ releases a draft publication;
  _Withdraw from the public site_ takes it off the site (with a reason), and _Publish again_ puts it
  back. Messages that cite the publication keep working either way.
* _Edit public period_ sets when the publication is on the public site. A start in the future is
  allowed: the publication is prepared now and appears on the day. A publication with no end stays
  on the site until somebody ends the period.
+
image::PublicationPublicPeriodDialog.png[Public Period, 380]
* *History* records what was done to the publication.
* _Edit_ opens the same drawer as on creation, and the cog menu _Publication actions_ offers _Move
  to domain…_.

[#publication-domains]
=== Domains: Owner, Citing and Moving

Every series and one-off publication has an _Owner domain_: the domain where it is listed and
administered, and whose time zone its cut-offs are read in. Only an administrator working in the
owner domain can change it.

Who may cite it is a separate setting, _Can be cited from messages in_:

image::PublicationAvailabilityField.png[Where a Publication Can Be Cited, 131]

* _This domain only_: only the owner domain can see and cite the publication.
* _Selected domains_: the domains you tick can cite it as well, but cannot change it.
* _Every domain_: every domain can cite it; only the owner can change it.

If you open a publication of another domain, for example from a link, Niord shows _Another domain's
publication_ and offers to switch to its domain:

image::PublicationForeignDomain.png[Another Domain's Publication, 800]

To give a publication to another domain, choose _Move to domain…_ in its cog menu, pick the domain
under _Move to_, type a reason and click _Move publication_. You need administrator rights in both
domains. From then on only the new domain can change the publication, and its future cut-offs are
read in the new domain's time zone; nothing already published changes.

[#publication-public-site]
=== What the Public Site Shows

An issue or one-off publication is listed on the public site when all three hold:

* its category has _Show on the public site_ ticked (see <<publication-categories>>);
* it is published, and not withdrawn;
* its public period has begun and has not ended.

The site's _Downloads_ page lists them on the tab _Active NtM and Publications_, grouped under
their categories in the categories' order. _Historical NtM and Publications_ lists the publications
of a date range, with a button for each of the last five years.

image::PublicSitePublications.png[Publications on the Public Site, 800]

_Download_ opens the issue's document. An issue generated from criteria also has _Browse_, which
shows the messages it holds on the public site's message pages:

image::PublicSitePublicationBrowse.png[Browsing an Issue on the Public Site, 800]

The page lists only the issue's messages, under their areas. The bar above the messages names the
issue, with _Download_ for its document and a close button (X) that goes back to the current list
of messages, and offers _Print_ and the areas the issue's messages are in, but not the other filters
of the message pages. _Details_, _List_ and _Map_ in the menu keep the issue while you browse it.

In the administration application, editors see which issues a message is in, and cite published
issues from the message editor; see
link:../editor-manual/manual.html#publication-editor-field[the Publications field] in the editor
manual.

[#publication-import-export]
=== Importing and Exporting Series Definitions

[[importing-and-exporting-publications]]
The cog menu _Publication actions_ on the _Publications_ page offers _Export series definitions_
and _Import series definitions_. They move the configuration of series between installations, for
example from a test system to production.

The file holds the definitions of the series and nothing else: no issues and no categories. It is
therefore not a backup of what has been published.

image::PublicationImportSeriesConfirm.png[Import Series Definitions, 240]

Importing asks first, because the file rewrites every series it names, field for field, leaving
only its status alone. A series whose category does not exist in this installation is skipped with
a line in the batch job's log. Click _Choose a file_ and pick the file. The import runs as the
batch job _publication-series-import_, which system administrators can follow on the
link:../sysadmin-manual/manual.html#batch-jobs[Batch Jobs] page. Nothing is activated by an import:
activate the imported series yourself once you have checked them.

An example of one series in the file, a monthly overview generated from the messages published in
each month. A moment in the file, such as `firstIssueStartsAt` (where the first content period
opens, see <<publication-series-frequency>>), is written with its offset from UTC.

[source,json]
----
[
  {
    "seriesId": "monthly-overview",
    "categoryId": "notices-to-mariners",
    "descs": [
      {
        "lang": "en",
        "name": "Monthly overview",
        "nameSuggestionPattern": "Overview ${month}/${year}",
        "fileNamePattern": "Overview-${year}-${month-2-digits}.pdf"
      }
    ],
    "status": "ACTIVE",
    "contentMode": "GENERATED_FROM_QUERY",
    "cadence": "MONTHLY",
    "nominalCutoffDayOfMonth": 1,
    "nominalCutoffTime": "12:00",
    "numberingScheme": "MONTH_YEAR",
    "timeRelation": "PUBLISHED_IN_INTERVAL",
    "aliveAtCutoff": true,
    "firstIssueStartsAt": "2026-01-01T00:00:00.000+00:00",
    "criteria": {
      "schemaVersion": 1,
      "match": "ALL",
      "criteria": [
        { "kind": "messageSeries", "operator": "IN", "values": ["example-nm"] }
      ]
    },
    "domainId": "example-nm",
    "availability": "ALL_DOMAINS",
    "languages": ["en"],
    "reportId": "standard",
    "pageSize": "A4",
    "pageOrientation": "PORTRAIT",
    "mapThumbnails": false,
    "messagePublication": "NONE",
    "releaseMode": "MANUAL_GATE",
    "cutoffDefault": "RELEASE_MOMENT",
    "nextIssueCreation": "AUTO_ON_PUBLISH",
    "languageSpecific": false
  }
]
----

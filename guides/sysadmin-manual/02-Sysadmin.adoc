[#sysadmin-page]
== Sysadmin Page

The _Sysadmin_ area is where system administrators set up what the rest of Niord works with: the
message series and the domains, the promulgation types, the firing schedules, the dictionaries and
the parameter types of the message templates, the script resources, the categories, the print
reports, the mail queue, the settings and the batch jobs.

To open it, click *Sysadmin* in the main menu. The item is in the menu only when you hold the system
administrator role in the domain that is selected in the header's domain menu: roles are given per
domain (see <<keycloak-integration,Keycloak Integration>>). When you switch to a domain where you do
not hold the role, *Sysadmin* leaves the menu, and an address of the area opened there takes you to
the front page with the message "Access denied.". Most of what you set up here is shared by every
domain, so a user who is a system administrator in one domain is normally made one in all of them.

image::SysadminPage.png[The Sysadmin overview, 800]

The area opens on its *Overview*: a short introduction and a card for each section. Click a card to
open the section. The same sections are listed in the rail on the left, which stays in place while
you work; in a narrow window the rail becomes a row of tabs above the page. The rail's *Batch Jobs*
entry shows how many batch jobs are running.

[cols="25,75", options="header"]
|===
| Section
| What it holds

| <<message-series,Message Series>>
| How messages are numbered and given their short ID, for each series of messages.

| <<domains,Domains>>
| The domains users work in, each a client of Keycloak, and what each domain may hold.

| <<promulgation-types,Promulgation Types>>
| The extra channels a message can be sent through, such as NAVTEX and audio.

| <<firing-schedules,Firing Schedules>>
| The schedules that turn firing periods into firing exercise warnings.

| <<dictionaries,Dictionaries>>
| The translatable texts of Niord: interface texts, texts of messages, mails and reports, and the
  values of template parameter lists.

| <<parameter-types,Parameter Types>>
| The kinds of parameters editors fill in when they execute a message template.

| <<script-resources,Script Resources>>
| The Freemarker templates and JavaScript files used by message templates, print reports and mails.

| <<categories,Categories>>
| The message categories and the message templates.

| <<print-reports,Print Reports>>
| The PDF reports of message lists.

| <<mail-queue,Mail Queue>>
| The mails Niord has sent or is about to send.

| <<settings,Settings>>
| The system settings.

| <<batch-jobs,Batch Jobs>>
| The background jobs, such as imports, and their logs.
|===

The *Niord Integration* section, which imports messages from another Niord server, is listed only
where the setting `adminIntegrationPageEnabled` is on (see <<integration,Integration>>).

[#sysadmin-lists]
=== Working with the Sysadmin Lists

The sections of the area work alike, so this is said once here:

* A section opens on a list. The *Filter* field above it narrows the list as you type; the chapter
  of each section says what the filter matches. The cross in the field clears it.
* The button on the right of the toolbar adds a row, such as *New Message Series*.
* Each row has its actions at the right end: the pencil edits the row, the double square copies it
  (where the section offers a copy), and the bin deletes it. A delete asks first, and *Yes* deletes.
* Adding, copying and editing open a panel on the right of the page, the _editor drawer_, with the
  row's fields. Its save button, such as *Save Message Series*, stays disabled until you change
  something and the required fields are filled in; *Cancel* closes the drawer. A copy opens as a new
  row with the copied values. Its identifier must be unique: most sections leave it empty for you
  to give, while a dictionary entry keeps the key and a script resource the folder of its path, for
  you to change. If
  you close the drawer with changes that are not saved, Niord asks "You have unsaved changes. Leave
  without saving?" first.
* Where a section can export and import its rows, the cog at the right end of the toolbar opens its
  action menu with *Export...* and *Import...*. *Export...* downloads the rows as a JSON file.
  *Import...* opens a dialog where you choose a JSON file (*Choose file…*, or drop it on the dialog).
  In most sections the import starts as soon as the file is chosen and runs in the background as a
  batch job; the dialog shows the upload result, and most sections add a link, *View batch job*, to
  the job's page under <<batch-jobs,Batch Jobs>>. The Dictionaries and Script Resources sections
  show what an import would change before anything is written. What an import does to rows that
  exist already differs from section to section, and each chapter says it.

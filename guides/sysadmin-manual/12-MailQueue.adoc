[#mail-queue]
== Mail Queue

Niord does not send a mail at once: it puts it in its _mail queue_ and sends it from there. The _Mail Queue_ page
lets a system administrator look through every mail in the queue, sent or not. You open it from *Sysadmin* ->
*Mail Queue*. Besides showing the mails, the page can send a held, sent or failed mail again (see
<<send-again,Sending a Mail Again>>); it cannot change or delete a mail.

image::MailQueuePage.png[Mail Queue, 800]

The picture shows the list filtered on the subject of one mail (see <<mail-filtering,Mail Filtering>>).

Mails in Niord come from a few places:

. An editor mails a selection of messages from the _Messages_ page, as described in the
  link:../editor-manual/manual.html#e-mail-selection-action[E-mail Selection Action] section of the editors'
  manual.
. An editor who writes a comment to a message can mail it to recipients, as described in the
  link:../editor-manual/manual.html#comments-editor-page[Comments Editor Page] section.
. Most mails are sent by mailing lists, as described in the <<mailing-lists,Mailing Lists>> chapter.

Niord deletes a mail from the queue when it is older than the number of days in the setting `mailDeleteAfterDays`
(30 unless it is changed), counted from when the mail was put in the queue. The page says so above the list ("NB:
Mails get deleted after 30 days."). With a value of 0 or less, Niord deletes no mail, and the page shows no such
note. A _Pending_ mail is never deleted, however old, so a mail sent again is not deleted while it waits to be sent;
a mail of any other status, _Held_ included, is deleted by its age.

[#sending-mails]
=== Sending Mails

A new mail in the queue is _Pending_. Every minute, Niord sends the pending mails that are due, at most as many as
the setting `mailMaxPerMinute` allows. A mail the mail server accepts becomes _Sent_. When sending fails, Niord
keeps the error with the mail and tries again later: after 1, 5, 15 and then 60 minutes. When the fifth attempt
fails too, the mail becomes _Error_, and Niord does not try again.

So check the queue now and then for mails with the status _Error_, and put right what caused them, such as a
mistyped address of a user or a contact. A failed mail can then be sent again (see <<send-again,Sending a Mail
Again>>).

A mail that the setting `mailValidRecipients`, the mail filter (see below), kept from its recipients becomes _Held_
instead of _Sent_. While the setting is `LOG`, nothing leaves the server: a pending mail that is due becomes _Held_,
and the server log has a line "Held, not sent (mailValidRecipients is LOG)" for it. While it is a list of addresses,
a mail with a recipient who is not on the list becomes _Held_ too, although a copy went to the list's first address.
A held mail stays held. Changing the setting later sends none of the held mails by itself; only _Send again_ does,
one mail or many at a time. A mail still _Pending_ when the setting is changed is sent as usual.

A mailing-list trigger that fails also leaves a mail with the status _Error_, which is never sent. Its subject
starts "Not sent: mailing list", and names the list and either the message (a status change trigger) or the trigger
(a scheduled trigger); its last error says why it failed. A scheduled trigger is tried again within the hour, and
leaves at most one such mail a day while it keeps failing.

After a restart, the server builds its text search index again, which takes some minutes on a large database. A
scheduled trigger whose message query has a text term (`query=`) and that runs in those minutes would find only the
messages indexed so far, so in the first half hour after the restart it waits, and is tried again every two
minutes. It leaves one mail with the status _Error_, which is never sent, whose subject starts "Delayed: mailing
list" and names the list and the trigger, and whose last error says that the text search index is being built again
and by when the mail is made, at the latest half an hour after the restart; the server log has one WARN saying the
trigger waits for the index. The trigger's mail is made as soon as the index is complete. When the index is still
not complete half an hour after the restart, the mail is sent all the same, and the log has a WARN naming the
trigger, since the mail may list too few messages. The server log shows when the index is complete: the first time
after the restart, it has the INFO line "Message search index complete: Lucene, <n> messages indexed in this fill".
A system administrator can also ask at any time at `GET /rest/monitor/message-index`, which answers, for example,
`{"lucene":true,"postgres":true,"complete":true}`; `lucene` is the index these triggers wait for. Where several
servers run, each answers for itself.

The index is also built again when a system administrator changes the setting
`messageIndexIncludeDeletedMessages` (see <<settings-from-code,Settings from Code>>). The triggers do not wait then,
since the half hour counts from the start of the server: a scheduled trigger with a text term that runs while the
index is being built is mailed all the same, with the WARN above, and its mail may list too few messages. So change
that setting outside the hours such triggers run.

Niord notes every status change of a message in its database, together with the change, until the mails of the
status change triggers are in the queue. So a status change trigger makes its mails even when the server restarts
just after the change: at once when the server is up again, or within a minute. Niord also notes, for each status
change, which of its triggers are done: their mails are in the queue, or their failure is (the mail with the status
_Error_ above). When a trigger is neither, for example because the database could not be reached for a moment, the
status change is kept and tried again a few minutes later, and only the triggers not yet done are run again: the
mailing lists that got their mail do not get it twice. When the server stops while it is putting a status change's
mails in the queue, that status change is taken again within a few minutes (ten when the stop cut a trigger short),
in the same way. A status change taken
again is mailed for the status it had, so its mail can arrive after the mail of a later status change of the same
message, for example a cancellation. A status change that could not be finished in five attempts (the server
stopped, a trigger hung, or the database could not be reached, each time) is given up: each of its status change
triggers not yet done leaves a mail with the status _Error_, as above, whose subject names its mailing list and whose
last error says it was given up, and the server log has an ERROR naming the message. When even that mail cannot be
saved, Niord tries five more times, minutes apart, and after that once an hour: the status change is kept until the
mail with the status _Error_ is saved, and each try leaves an ERROR in the server log naming the trigger, its mailing
list and the message.

The setting `mailValidRecipients` decides who Niord really sends mail to (see <<settings,Settings>>). It is read
for each mail when Niord sends it, not when the mail is made:

* `ALL` sends every mail to its recipients.
* A comma-separated list of addresses sends a recipient on the list the mail as it is; every other recipient's copy
  still goes to the first address of the list instead, with the original address in brackets as its name. The mail
  becomes _Sent_ only when every recipient (To, Cc and Bcc) was on the list; otherwise it becomes _Held_, since the
  filter kept it from its own recipients. This is the way to try the mails out on a test address, and on a
  deployment whose setting is a list, most mails therefore show as _Held_.
* `LOG`, the value a new deployment starts with, sends no mail at all: Niord writes a line in the server log, and the
  mail becomes _Held_. Set the setting to `ALL` when the deployment is to send mail; the mails held until then stay
  held until they are sent again.
* An empty value sends nothing either, but as a failure: every attempt fails, and the mail becomes _Error_ after the
  fifth.

A mail with some recipients on the list and some not is _Held_, although the recipients on the list did get it. When
it is sent again after the setting is opened, those recipients get it a second time; the confirmation lists every
recipient of the mails before they are sent. A mail that an earlier version of Niord, before _Held_ existed, marked
_Sent_ under a list cannot be told apart from a delivered one.

Niord reads `mailValidRecipients` for every mail it sends, so a change to it applies within a minute.
`mailDeleteAfterDays` is read at the clean-up, which runs once a day early in the morning, so a change to it deletes
mails from the next clean-up on, though the note above the list shows the new value as soon as the page is opened
again. A change to `mailMaxPerMinute` or `mailSender` takes effect at once: the next mail is sent from the new
sender, and the next minute's sending sends at most the new number.

[#mail-filtering]
=== Mail Filtering

The queue may hold thousands of mails, so the list shows 20 mails at a time, with a pager below it. The line above
the list says how many mails match the filter. The _Refresh_ button at the right, two circling arrows, searches
again with the same filter; the list does not refresh by itself.

The fields above the list filter it:

[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Filter
| Description

| Recipient
| Matches the addresses of the recipients.

| Sender
| Matches the address of the sender.

| Subject
| Matches the subject of the mails.

| All, Pending, Sent, Held, Error
| Shows the mails of one status, or of all of them. _Held_ shows the mails the setting `mailValidRecipients` kept
  from their recipients: from all of them while it was `LOG`, or from those not on the list while it was a list of
  addresses.

| From date, To date
| The two date fields with a calendar mark, after the status buttons: the first is the _From date_, the second the
  _To date_. They show the mails put in the queue within these dates. Either can be left empty.

|===

The list shows when each mail was put in the queue, its status, its sender, its first recipient (and how many
others it has) and its subject.

[#mail-details]
=== Mail Details

Click the *Open mail details* button (the envelope) at the end of a row to see the whole mail: its sender and
recipients, when it was put in the queue and, by its status, when it is due to be sent (_Due_, for a pending
mail; after a failed attempt, the time of the next one; for a held mail, the time it was due when it was held),
when it was sent (_Sent_) or when the last attempt was made (_Last attempt_, for a mail with the status _Error_; a
mailing-list trigger's failure record shows no such row, since it was never tried), when and by whom it was last
sent again (_Sent again_, only for a mail that was), the last error and the number of attempts when sending failed,
its subject and its content.

[#send-again]
=== Sending a Mail Again

A system administrator can send a held, sent or failed mail again. _Send again_ puts the same mail back in the queue
as _Pending_, exactly as it was made: the same subject, text and recipients. Niord sends it within a minute or two,
with the full set of attempts of a new mail, and the setting `mailValidRecipients` in force at that moment decides
who gets it. The mail's details then show when and by whom it was sent again, and the server log has a line for
every _Send again_ with the user, the mails sent again and how many of them were _Held_, _Sent_ or _Error_.

Two buttons send mails again:

* *Send again*, the paper plane at the end of a row, before the envelope, sends that one mail. It is there for a
  mail with the status _Held_, _Sent_ or _Error_. For a mail already sent, the confirmation says that its recipients
  get it a second time; a sent mail is sent again only this way, one at a time.
* *Send all matching again...*, at the right end of the filters, before the _Refresh_ button, sends every mail
  that matches the filter, on every page of the list. It is there only while the filter shows _Held_ or _Error_
  mails, and it takes at most 500 mails at a time: when more match, it sends nothing and asks you to narrow the
  search, for example with a _From date_ or a subject.

Before anything is sent, a confirmation lists the mails: one line for the mails with the same subject, status and
minute of creation, oldest first, with how many they are, when they were made and who they are to (for many mails,
the first ten addresses and how many more).

Some mails are not sent again:

* While `mailValidRecipients` is `LOG` or empty, _Send again_ is refused and nothing changes, since the mails would
  only be held or fail again. The page says why; set the setting on the _Settings_ page first.
* While `mailValidRecipients` is a list of addresses, a _Sent_ mail with a recipient who is not on the list is
  left as it was: it would become _Held_, and a later _Send again_ of the held mails would deliver it to its
  recipients a second time. The page says "The mail filter would keep this mail from its recipients. Open the filter
  first." A held or failed mail is sent again under the list as usual, and becomes _Sent_ or _Held_ again by the
  rule above.
* A mailing-list trigger's failure record ("Not sent: ...", "Delayed: ...", or a status change that was given up)
  is not a mail, and has no *Send again* button; _Send all matching again..._ leaves it out.
* A mail that changed after the list was read, because it was sent again by someone else, was being sent, or was
  deleted, is left as it was. The message after the confirmation says how many mails were queued and how many were
  left.

Mails sent again go out at the pace of the setting `mailMaxPerMinute`, after the mails Niord makes itself: 500 mails
at 10 a minute take 50 minutes, and a new warning's mails are not held back by them. A held mail that is never sent
again is deleted by its age, like any other mail.

[#send-again-after-a-restore]
==== After a Restore

A deployment started on a copy of another Niord's database finds that system's pending mails in the queue. To choose
which of them to send:

. Start the deployment with `mailValidRecipients` at `LOG`, and let the sender job run (it is switched off by
  `-Dniord.schedule.mail-send-cron=-`): the pending mails that are due become _Held_. The sender job takes at most
  `mailMaxPerMinute` mails a minute (10 unless it is changed), so a large queue becomes _Held_ over a while: 300
  mails take half an hour. With the sender job switched off they stay _Pending_, and all of them go out by
  themselves once the job runs with the setting open.
. Before you change the setting, filter the list on _Pending_ and wait until no mail that is due is left (a mail's
  details show when it is due). A pending mail still there when the setting is changed is sent by itself, under the
  new setting.
. To try it out first, set `mailValidRecipients` to a test address and send one held mail again: it arrives at the
  test address, and it becomes _Held_ again (unless the test address was its only recipient), so it is sent with the
  other held mails later.
. Set `mailValidRecipients` to `ALL`.
. Filter the list on _Held_ and a _From date_, and send the mails again with _Send all matching again..._. Leave out
  the mails made before the copy was taken: the old system may have sent them after that.

A mail the old system marked _Sent_ while its own setting was `LOG` or a list of addresses reads as _Sent_ here too,
since it cannot be told apart from a delivered one; send it again one at a time if it is needed.
